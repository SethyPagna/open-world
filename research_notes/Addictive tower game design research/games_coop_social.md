# Co-op, Party and Social-Deduction / Betrayal Games: Why They Are Fun, Viral and Sticky (research as of Sept 2026)

Scope: Among Us, Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., PEAK, Chained Together, Fall Guys, Goose Goose Duck, Liar's Bar, Buckshot Roulette, Content Warning, Dark and Darker, Dread Hunger, Deceit, Human Fall Flat, It Takes Two / Split Fiction, Deep Rock Galactic, Helldivers 2, Getting Over It / Only Up, and Roblox social hits (Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, 99 Nights in the Forest).
Every section ends with "Implication for our game". "Our game" means the browser (Three.js) tower game for 1 to 8 players, with bots filling empty seats: lettered doors to sub-levels, gimmick rooms, powers, teaming up and betrayal, hub minigames, a skin shop, and layers that go Well, then Sky, then Space.

Source caveat: gamedeveloper.com, pushtotalk.gg and indiegame.com pages could not be fetched in this session. Facts from those outlets come from search-result summaries of their headlines or ledes and are marked "(via search summary)". Wikipedia pages were fetched directly.

## 1. What moment-to-moment mechanics generate emergent comedy and clips (physics, proximity voice chat, ragdolls, shared fate, blame)?

### Takeaway
The hits share one thing: the game creates a physical problem that everyone owns together, then lets clumsy physics, proximity voice and a clear chance to blame someone turn failure into a story. Examples are a chain linking all players, a stamina bar, fragile loot, or friendly fire that is always on. Developers say openly that the game is a stage and the friends supply the comedy.

### Cited Findings
**Shared-fate constraints (the strongest comedy engine)**
- Chained Together: players are chained together and must climb out of hell. Critics compared it to QWOP, Getting Over It and Only Up!, and said the chain is what makes it "stand apart". Keeping the team coordinated is the main challenge. It has 10 "Wings" power-ups that let you recover from a fall, a hard mode with rising lava, and an easy mode with checkpoints — [Wikipedia: Chained Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)
- PEAK: a first-person climb for up to 4 players with proximity chat. The stamina bar is shrunk by hunger, injury, item weight, poison and temperature, so one player's bad choice (eating the wrong thing, carrying too much) becomes the group's problem. Dead teammates come back through items or altars at the end of each level. An enemy (the "Scoutmaster") appears when the group spreads about 200 m apart, which pushes players to stay together — [Wikipedia: Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game))
- Lethal Company: the team must hit a profit quota each 3-day cycle or lose the run, and must get back to the ship before midnight or lose the scrap they collected. The team wins or loses together — [Wikipedia: Lethal Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)

**Physics and fragile objects**
- R.E.P.O.: players carry valuables with physics to extraction points. Damaging an item lowers its value. Players say the game gets funnier with more people and they enjoy "setting up each other for the fall" — [TheFPSReview](https://www.thefpsreview.com/2025/03/25/r-e-p-o-is-a-10-early-access-comedy-horror-game-with-up-to-six-players-that-has-engaged-over-a-quarter-million-players-in-just-one-month/). PC Gamer singled out its physics system as the edge over Lethal Company — [Wikipedia: R.E.P.O.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.)
- Human: Fall Flat, a wobbly-physics puzzle platformer, has sold more than 55 million units. It grew from 8 maps to 28, plus many cosmetics, over about a decade — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/human-fall-flat/sales-55-million); [Curve Games](https://curvegames.com/news/human-fall-flat-hits-55-millions-units/)

**Proximity voice**
- Lethal Company: voice is proximity-limited by default, so you can only talk to nearby players unless you find walkie-talkies — [Game Design Library](https://www.gamedesignlibrary.com/post/proximity-chat-changes-the-game-how-lethal-company-s-game-design-innovated-multiplayer-horror-games)
- The friendslop label, as summarized on Wikipedia, lists these shared traits: low barrier to entry, team goals, extraction loops, proximity chat, physics-based interaction, low-fidelity 3D, a price of about $20 or less, and "often unenjoyable when played solo" — [Wikipedia: Friendslop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop)
- Goose Goose Duck launched with proximity voice before Among Us added it. Proximity voice is available in private games, but it was not added to public games — [Steam discussion / GGD community (via search summary)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1568590/discussions/0/5501694039539892580/)

**Friendly fire and blame as comedy**
- Helldivers 2 keeps friendly fire always on. Arrowhead CEO Johan Pilestedt said "The chuckles that happen when you accidentally carpet bomb your friends allows for a playfulness that in reality would be a tragedy" — [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/helldivers-2-ceo-johan-pilestedt-knows-we-may/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2s-friendly-fire-and-killer-bugs-show-the-fragility-of-humans-and-are-also-just-funny-creative-director-says/). He also argued for it on grounds of consistency: if friendly fire went off, bullet damage against enemies would have to go off too — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/139269/helldivers-2-friendly-fire-stays-on)

**Designers expect friends to supply the comedy**
- Caelan Pollock (Aggro Crab, who pitched PEAK) defined friendslop as "something that assumes that you and your friends will make up the difference in comedy and interaction in what the game is lacking" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-was-meant-to-be-friendslop-game-from-the-start)
- Coverage of the genre says streamers took to Lethal Company because of its "capacity for emergent narratives ranging from terrifying to hilarious" — [Know Your Meme friendslop explainer (via search summary)](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/whats-the-term-for-games-like-lethal-company-and-repo-people-calling-co-op-games-friendslop-explained)

**Filming as a mechanic (the clip is inside the loop)**
- Content Warning: players find creepy things, film them before they run out of oxygen or die, then escape and upload the footage to "go viral" in-game. The core loop makes shareable clips — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/horror/april-fools-phenom-content-warning-draws-in-more-than-204000-concurrent-players/); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/02/content-warning-140k-ccu-4-5-million-downloads-landfall)

### Inferences
- Comedy comes from three ingredients together. **Coupling:** my mistake hurts you (chain, stamina, fragile cargo, friendly fire). **Visible cause:** everyone can see who did it (blame). **Low cost:** the run keeps going or restarts fast. Take away coupling and the physics is just clumsy. Take away blame and there is no story. Make the cost too high and people rage instead of laugh.
- Implication for our game (mechanics): give each sub-level room at least one **coupling gimmick** in the tower's own style. Examples: a "Bound Pair" floor that tethers players; a "Shared Breath" room with one oxygen pool for the whole party; a "Porcelain Idol" clear condition where you carry a fragile relic to the exit; a "Mirror Curse" where one player's jump moves another. This matches the weird non-combat clear conditions of *The Tutorial Is Too Hard* style and generates comedy.
- Implication (physics): give characters ragdoll or wobble on hits, knockback and falls, and make pushing and grabbing other players a core verb. Three.js with a lightweight physics engine (Rapier or cannon-es) can do this in the browser. Bots should ragdoll just as badly as humans, so solo sessions also produce funny moments.
- Implication (blame surfaces): show a short "who caused it" tag or instant replay when someone falls or a room fails, for example "Pushed by Minji" or "Dropped the Idol: Bot-Kang". Chained Together and REPO get laughs because the cause is readable.
- Implication (voice): proximity voice is central to the Western hits, but a browser game with strangers and bots needs a fallback. Use proximity text bubbles, emotes and a quick-chat or ping wheel for strangers. Keep optional WebRTC proximity voice for friend lobbies only (the GGD pattern: proximity voice in private games, not public ones).
- Implication (clips): copy Content Warning. Turn "being watched" into a mechanic. The Korean tower genre already has watchers or sponsors observing climbers. Let spectacular fails, clutch saves and betrayals earn "Audience" or "Sponsor" points, and keep a rolling replay buffer so players can save and share the last 10 to 15 seconds.

### Gaps
- No developer postmortem from semiwork (R.E.P.O.) on physics tuning for comedy was found.
- A widely repeated Zeekerss "design philosophy" quote about imagination and friends screaming comes only from a low-quality SEO site (fearing.org). It was left out as unverified.
- No quantitative data (for example, the share of clips featuring physics fails) was found.

## 2. How do successful betrayal games keep betrayal fun (low stakes, short rounds, fast re-queue, reveals, laughter) and avoid toxicity? What goes wrong in games where betrayal feels bad?

### Takeaway
Betrayal feels funny when it is expected, performed as part of the game, and repeatable. It works when the round is short, the stakes are reset next round, players are friends (so trust is high and rebuilds between rounds), and the rules frame lying as the point of the game. Betrayal feels bad with strangers, in long matches, when the outcome is permanent, when it costs real money or progress, and when the game gives griefers tools and no guardrails. Several betrayal games (Dread Hunger, Deceit) were killed mainly by cheaters, DDoS and server costs, not only by their design.

### Cited Findings
**Why betrayal is fun**
- An Among Us 3D case study found that betrayal is "funnier with friends" because trust is high, so there is "more room to fall from grace." Breaking trust is "performative, acceptable, and expected," and players "build more trust in each other between games by laughing about their betrayal." Players come back between matches to "retell and reinterpret their deception." They consistently had the most fun with established friends — [Digital Thriving Playbook: Make Trust, Break Trust](https://digitalthrivingplaybook.org/example/make-trust-break-trust-unlocking-prosocial-potential-in-among-us-3d/)
- Framing matters: Innersloth worried that a crewmate role able to kill once per game would be "a dream for bad faith actors." They named and styled it "Vigilante", with a justice theme, and players then described it with "themes of justice" rather than griefing — [Digital Thriving Playbook](https://digitalthrivingplaybook.org/example/make-trust-break-trust-unlocking-prosocial-potential-in-among-us-3d/)
- Among Us is structured so the betrayal is contained. Any living player can call a meeting (by reporting a body or pressing the emergency button). Players discuss and vote someone out. Dead players become ghosts who still do tasks or sabotage, so being killed does not remove you from the game — [Wikipedia: Among Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)
- Among Us matches usually last about 10 to 15 minutes, occasionally up to about 30 — [The Global Gaming (low-authority source)](https://theglobalgaming.com/gaming/average-match-time-length-among-us)
- In Liar's Bar, lying is the rule, not a hidden role. In Liar's Deck, a player caught lying must play Russian roulette with a revolver. In Liar's Dice, they drink from a jar of poison. Each round ends when someone is caught — [Wikipedia: Liar's Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar). It won the 2024 Steam Award for Most Innovative Gameplay — [Wikipedia: Liar's Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar)
- Buckshot Roulette: players take turns choosing to shoot themselves or the opponent with a shotgun loaded with a mix of live and blank shells. Defibrillator charges act as lives (1, then 3, then 4 across three rounds), random items add bluffing and strategy, and a 4-player multiplayer mode was added on 31 Oct 2024 — [Wikipedia: Buckshot Roulette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckshot_Roulette)
- Extraction games: one essay argues the *lack* of a reputation system is what makes extraction betrayal exciting, since any encounter can go sideways — [Big Picture Mode substack](https://bigpicturemode.substack.com/p/humanity-first-unless-youve-got-something)

**Toxicity and what goes wrong**
- Online social deduction with public lobbies "has to deal with toxicity as a major issue, partly due to the lack of pre-existing trust between the players." The article argues toxicity can be tackled through UX and communication design — [Game Developer: From Mafia to Among Us (via search summary)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/from-mafia-to-among-us-can-social-deduction-evolve-as-online-multiplayer-)
- Among Us public games relying on text chat limit social depth compared with private voice lobbies. Suggested fixes are more defined roles, to reduce dependence on communication quality — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/06/among-us-critical-play-of-social-deduction/)
- Among Us, October 2020: a hacker ("Eris Loris") spammed lobbies. Claims said 4.9 million players in 1.5 million games were affected — [Wikipedia: Among Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)
- Dread Hunger (8 players, 2 secret cannibal "Thralls"): released 26 Jan 2022, sold over 1 million copies, Metacritic 73 ("mixed or average") — [Wikipedia: Dread Hunger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dread_Hunger). All-time peak was about 107,494 CCU; it now has a few hundred players. Official servers were shut down because of "frequent and severe DDOS attacks" and server costs (announced 13 Nov 2023, delisted 1 Dec 2023, servers off 1 Jan 2024) — [Steam Charts / Dread Hunger data (via search summary)](https://steamcharts.com/app/1418630)
- Deceit (2017) shut down on Steam on 6 Nov 2025, citing DDoS, cheaters, privacy regulations and an aging engine. Deceit 2 launched "too early" and had to be relaunched — [Delisted Games](https://delistedgames.com/deceit-shutting-down-on-steam-on-november-6th/)
- Dark and Darker: players complain that single-use extractions "punish" duos and trios, which pushes teammates to split up and exit separately, even though duo and trio stacks make up about 60% of modes — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2016590/discussions/0/6513974610099203150/)
- Steal a Brainrot (Roblox) is built on stealing other players' characters from their bases (capture-the-flag style), with shields you activate by button. Viral content centered on children crying after their brainrots were stolen. Critics called it pay-to-win because the best defensive items are for Robux only — [Wikipedia: Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot). In Grow a Garden, Robux can be used to "steal others' crops" — [Wikipedia: Grow a Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)

**The cooperative counter-model**
- Deep Rock Galactic devs: "I don't know how many other games are out there where you can just jump in, play with strangers, and have a great experience." The "Rock and Stone" salute was a deliberate early design choice — [PC Gamer (via search summary)](https://www.pcgamer.com/meet-one-of-the-last-positive-communities-left-in-gaming/); [Z League (secondary)](https://www.zleague.gg/theportal/deep-rock-galactic-rock-and-stone-embracing-the-dwarven-spirit-together/)

### Inferences
- A pattern runs across all of these games: betrayal is fun when its **blast radius is small** (one round, one room, a few minutes), when it is **announced by the rules** (Liar's Bar: everyone lies; Among Us: you know there are impostors), when there is a **reveal moment** (vote results, the revolver click, the shell), and when **the victim stays in the game** (Among Us ghosts, and a quick return). It feels bad when it wipes out long-term investment, happens deep in a long match (Dread Hunger matches are long survival sessions; exact length is unverified), is sold for money (Brainrot and Grow a Garden stealing with Robux), or comes from strangers the victim cannot laugh with later.
- Implication for our game (betrayal rules):
  1. **Betray only inside a sub-level.** Stolen loot, a sabotaged clear or a knocked-off-ledge ally costs that room or that floor's reward, never persistent powers, account progress or skins.
  2. **Keep betrayal rounds short** (3 to 6 minute rooms), with a scripted **"Reveal"** at the room exit: slow-motion replay, "Traitor: X" stinger, and a crowd-reaction sound. The reveal is the punchline.
  3. **Make betrayal part of the rules.** Some doors, such as a "Liar's Room" or "Two Keys, One Exit", openly say that only one person can win. Others give hidden-role twists. Players should never feel tricked by the game itself, only by other players.
  4. **Keep victims in play.** Downed or betrayed players become ghosts, heckling spirits or "sponsors" who can nudge traps, vote or bet on outcomes (the Among Us ghost-task model). No dead time.
  5. **Name and theme sabotage heroically or comically** ("Trickster", "Gambler", "Vigilante"), not sadistically, following the Innersloth framing lesson.
  6. **Separate lobby types.** Full betrayal kit in friend and private lobbies. In public or stranger lobbies, restrict or telegraph betrayal (announced "Traitor Floors", cooldowns, visible intent). Bots should be the main betrayers in public play so humans are not griefed by humans.
  7. **Never sell sabotage.** The skin shop stays cosmetic. The Brainrot and Grow a Garden pay-to-steal model goes viral through crying kids but is the most toxic pattern here.
  8. **Make co-op the default and betrayal the spice.** DRG shows that interdependence plus shared rituals (a salute or emote) builds a community of strangers who play well together. The tower's default tone should be "climb together", with betrayal as a tempting, costly option on some doors.
- Implication (operations): Dread Hunger and Deceit died from DDoS, cheaters and server costs. For a browser game, plan for authoritative server validation of betrayal actions, cheap relay servers and anti-spam from day one.

### Gaps
- No primary data was found on Dread Hunger's match length or on quantified toxicity reports. Its decline is documented (peak about 107k, then shutdown), but how much toxicity contributed, compared with DDoS and hacking, is not documented in the sources found.
- No sources were found on Goose Goose Duck's moderation or anti-toxicity systems. The Forbes article was blocked (HTTP 403).
- No published retention comparison between friend lobbies and stranger lobbies in social deduction games was found.

## 3. What session length, lobby flow and "time to fun" do these games have? How fast is the first laugh?

### Takeaway
The hit loops are built from short, repeatable units: a 1 to 5 minute Fall Guys round, a 10 to 15 minute Among Us match, an 11:40 Lethal Company day. These nest inside a larger arc: a 5-round Fall Guys show, a 3-day Lethal Company quota, PEAK's 6-biome climb. The first funny moment comes from the first shared physical interaction, not from a tutorial. No hard "time to first laugh" metric is published.

### Cited Findings
- Fall Guys: each match has 5 rounds, each lasting about 1 to 5 minutes — [Screen Rant (via search summary)](https://screenrant.com/fall-guys-rounds-each-game-match-mediatonic/)
- Lethal Company: an in-game day lasts 700 seconds (11 min 40 s) of real time, and a quota cycle gives 3 days of exploring. So one quota is about 35 to 40 minutes plus ship time, and a run is a chain of quotas until one is failed — [Lethal Company Wiki: Time](https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/Time); [Wikipedia: Lethal Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)
- Among Us: about 10 to 15 minutes per match, sometimes 1 to 30 minutes — [The Global Gaming (low-authority)](https://theglobalgaming.com/gaming/average-match-time-length-among-us)
- Buckshot Roulette: a standard game is 3 short rounds, and an endless "Double or Nothing" mode lets players push their luck up to $70,000 — [Wikipedia: Buckshot Roulette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckshot_Roulette)
- PEAK: the climb covers 6 biome levels. The map rotates every 24 hours at 17:00 UTC (a daily shared seed), and players who reach the Peak unlock a harder difficulty and cosmetic "badges" for achievements — [Wikipedia: Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game))
- R.E.P.O.: a roguelike loop of randomized levels, with a "Service Station" shop between rounds to spend "SURPLUS" on upgrades, weapons and tools — [Wikipedia: R.E.P.O.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.)
- Only Up!: no checkpoints and no save system, so a fall costs all progress — [Wikipedia: Only Up!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_Up!). Chained Together added an easy mode with checkpoints and 10 recovery power-ups on top of its punishing base mode — [Wikipedia: Chained Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)
- Grow a Garden: crops keep growing while the player is offline. Seed stock rotates, and "exclusive items are released weekly that players must be online to claim, helping to boost player retention" — [Wikipedia: Grow a Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Fall Guys cut Solos lobbies from 40-60 players to 36-40 on 21 Feb 2023 to speed up matchmaking — [Fall Guys Wiki: Solos](https://fallguysultimateknockout.fandom.com/wiki/Solos)

### Inferences
- The shared structure is a short "atom" of 1 to 12 minutes that ends in a clear result (qualified or eliminated, quota met or not, vote result), a between-atom hub with a shop or upgrades (REPO Service Station, the Lethal Company ship), and a longer "arc" that gives the run meaning (quota chain, 6 biomes, 5 rounds). Players remember arcs but quit or continue at the end of each atom, so every atom must end on a hook.
- Implication for our game (pacing):
  - **Sub-level room (the atom):** 2 to 6 minutes, one gimmick, one clear condition, one punchline or reveal at the exit.
  - **Floor (the mini-arc):** 3 to 5 rooms through lettered doors, about 15 to 25 minutes, ending with a floor boss or gimmick monster and a hub return (shop, powers, minigames), like the REPO Service Station.
  - **Layer (the macro-arc):** Well, then Sky, then Space. This is the persistent progression over days and weeks. Like PEAK's biome list and the unlocked harder difficulty, each layer should look and feel different when you reach it.
  - **Daily "Tower Seed":** PEAK rotates its map daily at a fixed UTC time. A daily door layout gives streamers and friend groups a shared talking point ("did you get 3E today?").
- Implication (time to fun): the first 60 seconds must include a shared physical interaction with other players or bots, such as a push, a fall or a carried object, before any menu or text tutorial. Teach through the first room's gimmick. Grow a Garden's offline growth and Lethal Company's instant drop to the moon both skip setup.
- Implication (failure cost): Only Up's total-loss rule makes clips but is brutal. Chained Together's two tiers (punishing base mode, plus Wings and checkpoints in easy mode) show how to keep the rage-comedy while still letting casual groups progress. Offer "Hardcore Doors", where a fall sends you to the floor start, alongside normal doors with checkpoint relics.
- Implication (lobby flow): shrink or bot-fill lobbies rather than make players wait. Fall Guys cut lobby size for speed. Hub minigames should cover any matchmaking wait, and re-queue after a floor should take one click.

### Gaps
- No published first-laugh or time-to-fun metric (seconds from boot to first shared interaction) exists for any of these games.
- No authoritative median session length or runs-per-session figures were found for PEAK, REPO or Chained Together. The Among Us match length comes from a low-authority site.

## 4. Why did PEAK / R.E.P.O. / Lethal Company / Chained Together explode on Twitch/YouTube/TikTok; what makes a game streamable?

### Takeaway
They exploded because of four things together. They are **instantly readable** (climb up, carry loot out, don't die). Failure is **loud and visible** (falls, screams, ragdolls). They are **priced or distributed for group purchase** ($5 to $10, free days, PS Plus, Friend's Pass). And they were **seeded to streamers and clip culture** at launch. Streamers act as the proof that the game is fun with friends. Viewers then buy it to recreate what they watched with their own friends.

### Cited Findings
**Launch pattern and streamer seeding**
- Fall Guys: beta codes went to popular streamers on 24 July 2020, one day before open beta. Streamers passed codes on to their audiences, and demand forced a second beta. Launch was a free PS Plus game for a month. The game became the second most popular on Twitch and YouTube in its first month, with 113M hours viewed, and reached 17.9M PlayStation MAU in August — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2020/09/17/fall-guys). About 23M hours were watched on Twitch in week one, and it was the #1 category several times — [Screen Rant (via search summary)](https://screenrant.com/fall-guys-launch-week-stats-sales-twitch-view-hours/)
- Among Us: dev Forest Willard credited Sodapoppin with popularizing it on Twitch in July 2020, followed by xQc, Pokimane, Ninja and MrBeast. The October 2020 AOC and Ilhan Omar stream drew almost 700k concurrent viewers. The game first took off in Mexico, Brazil and South Korea — [Wikipedia: Among Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)
- Chained Together (released 19 June 2024): DisguisedToast, LilyPichu and others were streaming it within hours, reaching six figures in combined concurrent viewers — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/what-is-chained-together-new-only-up-style-co-op-game-2787791/). It won the 2024 Golden Joystick Streamers' Choice award and The Streamer Awards Stream Game of the Year — [Wikipedia: Chained Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)
- Lethal Company won Streamer Awards Stream Game of the Year (Feb 2024) and VTuber Awards Stream Game of the Year (Dec 2024) — [Wikipedia: Lethal Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)
- PEAK won Streamer Awards Stream Game of the Year, Golden Joystick Best Multiplayer and the Steam Awards "Better With Friends" award — [Wikipedia: Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game))
- Content Warning was free on Steam for 24 hours on 1 April 2024. People "woke up one day and suddenly they're just bombarded with a bunch of footage from a new game" — [Game Developer (via search summary)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/content-warning-opens-to-near-205k-players-nets-6-2m-owners-at-launch). It got 6.2M claims in 24 hours and peaked at 204,439 CCU — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/horror/april-fools-phenom-content-warning-draws-in-more-than-204000-concurrent-players/)
- Only Up!: about 85,000 concurrent Twitch viewers by late June 2023, driven by streamers rage-quitting after losing progress. A user-made Fortnite OnlyUp map then reached more than 100,000 players, eight times the original game's player count — [Wikipedia: Only Up!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_Up!)
- Goose Goose Duck hit a record Steam peak of 702,845 on 12 Jan 2023 (800k across PC and mobile), powered by China and Korea and a stream by BTS member V — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/powered-by-k-pop-an-among-us-like-has-smashed-among-us-concurrent-players-record/); [Game Developer (via search summary)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-goose-goose-duck-i-reaches-800-000-concurrent-players)
- Buckshot Roulette went viral on Twitch and TikTok after its itch.io release (28 Dec 2023). It was described as "an almost perfect game for streamers" — [Wikipedia: Buckshot Roulette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckshot_Roulette); [Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/viral-hit-buckshot-roulette-sells-over-one-million-copies/)

**Price and friction**
- Nick Kaman (Aggro Crab) on PEAK's price: "Eight bucks is still five bucks. It doesn't become ten bucks." $5 and $8 feel like the same mental price bracket — [Game File](https://www.gamefile.news/p/peak-interview)
- Friendslop games typically cost $20 or less and are meant as affordable social activities — [Wikipedia: Friendslop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop). Chained Together sells for $5 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together). Lethal Company costs $10 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company). R.E.P.O. is listed at $8 on Wikipedia and $9.99 by TheFPSReview — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.); [TheFPSReview](https://www.thefpsreview.com/2025/03/25/r-e-p-o-is-a-10-early-access-comedy-horror-game-with-up-to-six-players-that-has-engaged-over-a-quarter-million-players-in-just-one-month/) (conflicting; regional or launch pricing may differ)

**What makes a game streamable (design commentary)**
- A streamable moment requires "instant comprehension." Viewers should grasp, without captions, what the player wants, what can be lost, and what action decides the outcome. Games that produce short shareable moments (jump scares, absurd physics) feed TikTok, Shorts and X, which feed Twitch growth — [Nerdbot (industry blog, moderate authority)](https://nerdbot.com/2026/01/26/what-makes-a-game-streamable-in-10-seconds-designing-moments-that-work-for-shorts-and-reels/)
- Academic work on Twitch audience participation suggests short, independent rounds let newcomers (and viewers) join on equal footing — [arXiv: Audience and Streamer Participation at Scale on Twitch](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.00215)
- PEAK dev culture note: "Text is evil" was Aggro Crab and Landfall's lesson about how Discord text warped their team's communication during development. It is about how the team talked, not a documented in-game tutorial rule — [Game Developer (via search summary)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/-text-is-evil-how-making-peak-changed-indie-studio-aggro-crab)

### Inferences
- A streamable game has a **visible goal** (height, an exit, loot value), **visible loss** (fall distance, broken loot, death), **big reactions** (voice, screams, physics), **short independent rounds** (viewers can join mid-stream), **group-scale content** (streamer groups such as DisguisedToast and friends), and **low friction for viewers to copy it** (cheap, free days, one-owner passes).
- Implication for our game (presentation):
  - **Height is the progress bar.** Always show floor, layer and altitude on screen and in the HUD, like the PEAK and Only Up rise. Every viewer instantly understands "they're on Floor 7 of the Well".
  - **Loss must be visible and dramatic.** Long falls through the well shaft, a slow-motion betrayal replay, or a "Door 3E collapses" camera.
  - **The lettered door choice is a strong stream hook.** Chat can vote on which door (A, B or E) the streamer takes. Add an optional "Chat picks the door" or "Viewers are the Sponsors" mode, which fits the Korean-tower trope of watching gods.
  - **Browser means zero-install, free and linkable.** This is a Content Warning-level friction advantage. A streamer can drop a room link and viewers join instantly, with bots filling empty seats. Make "Join streamer's tower" a first-class flow.
  - **Seed creators before launch** with private tower codes (the Fall Guys model), and design at least one "streamer group" mode for 4 to 8 friends.
  - **Ship clip tools:** an auto-saved 15-second buffer, a "Moment" button, and vertical-friendly camera framing for TikTok and Shorts.

## 5. How do they handle solo players (bots) vs friends; which games failed because they need friends?

### Takeaway
Most friendslop hits are weak solo: Wikipedia's genre summary says they are "often unenjoyable when played solo". They rely on players bringing friends, on Discord communities, or on public lobbies. The games that handle solo well use **bots that fill lobbies and are tuned to lose early** (Fall Guys), **an explicit solo mode** (Chained Together), or **no need for other players at all** (Grow a Garden's AFK loop). Clear "died because it needed friends" cases are hard to prove. The documented shrinking and deaths (Fall Guys lobby cuts, Dread Hunger, Deceit) mix low population with cheating and server costs.

### Cited Findings
- Friendslop is "often unenjoyable when played solo" — [Wikipedia: Friendslop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop). Players without friends lean on Discords and community forums to find groups — [Friendslop coverage (via search summary)](https://www.stackup.org/post/friendslop-games-for-your-next-online-game-night)
- Split Fiction has no matchmaking, and the developers point players to Discord to find partners — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2001120/discussions/0/601898034735939873)
- Fall Guys at launch put bots in each player's first 10 matches, in small numbers and designed not to survive the first few rounds — [search summary of Prima Games / Dot Esports](https://primagames.com/tips/are-there-bots-in-fall-guys-answered). On 7 April 2022, bots were enabled in the Main Show to fill lobbies when too few players were available — [Fall Guys Wiki: Matchmaking](https://fallguysultimateknockout.fandom.com/wiki/Matchmaking). In February 2023, Solos lobbies were cut from 40-60 to 36-40 for faster matchmaking, a sign of a shrinking population — [Fall Guys Wiki: Solos](https://fallguysultimateknockout.fandom.com/wiki/Solos)
- Chained Together supports up to 4 players and has a single-player mode — [Wikipedia: Chained Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)
- PEAK: an enemy appears when the group gets about 200 m apart. The design assumes a group, and there is no mention of bots — [Wikipedia: Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game))
- Grow a Garden works almost without other players. Crops grow offline, it has an "AFK-friendly design", and it was called "the new generation's FarmVille" — [Wikipedia: Grow a Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Goose Goose Duck is free-to-play on Steam, iOS and Android, which widens the pool for public lobbies — [Game Developer (via search summary)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-goose-goose-duck-i-reaches-800-000-concurrent-players)
- Dread Hunger's community survives after shutdown through an unofficial Discord with a bot that creates lobbies for friends — [Dread Hunger data (via search summary)](https://steamdb.info/app/1418630/charts/)
- Deep Rock Galactic is an example of stranger play that works: "just jump in, play with strangers, and have a great experience" — [PC Gamer (via search summary)](https://www.pcgamer.com/meet-one-of-the-last-positive-communities-left-in-gaming/)

### Inferences
- Needing friends is both the viral engine (each buyer recruits 3 to 7 others) and the retention cliff (when the group stops playing, each member stops too). Bots are the bridge. Fall Guys' approach of hidden early bots tuned to lose, then bots as silent lobby fill, is the most-documented working pattern.
- Implication for our game (bots are a feature, not filler):
  1. **Rival Climbers with personalities.** Bots should be named characters from the tower's world, each with a visible personality: a coward, a schemer, a helper, a show-off. They use emotes and short voice-line barks or text barks, so a solo player still gets social comedy and betrayal drama.
  2. **Bots betray visibly and fairly.** Solo players meet betrayal from bots whose intent is telegraphed (a shifty emote, a whispered bark), so the first betrayal a new player experiences is funny and learnable, not a griefing human.
  3. **Tune bots to lose early and fail funny,** following the Fall Guys first-10-matches rule. New players should "win" their first rooms and see bots ragdoll off ledges.
  4. **Bots fill seats instantly, and humans replace bots mid-floor** (drop-in), so friends joining a link never wait.
  5. **Solo progress should not be second-class.** Persistent powers, layer progress and skins earn at the same rate solo or grouped, with a group bonus instead of a solo penalty. Also add an AFK or offline touch in the hub, such as a "garden" or "shrine" that grows between sessions (the Grow a Garden pattern).
  6. **Remove group friction:** one link to join, no account required to try, and guests allowed in a friend's party (the Friend's Pass idea translated to the browser).

### Gaps
- No postmortem was found that attributes a co-op game's death specifically to "needing friends". The evidence is inferential (Fall Guys lobby shrink, Dread Hunger and Deceit declines that also involved cheating and DDoS).
- It was not confirmed whether Lethal Company, REPO or PEAK offer public matchmaking. Their Wikipedia pages do not state it.
- Among Us bot support: no source confirming whether offline or bot play exists was retrieved.

### Inferences
- The virality drivers, in order of evidence strength:
  1. streamer groups seeded at launch;
  2. a short clip that explains itself;
  3. a group-buy price ($5 to $10) or free access (free day, PS Plus, F2P, Friend's Pass);
  4. a region or celebrity spark (GGD in China and Korea with BTS; Among Us in Latin America and Korea).
  A browser game is naturally free and zero-install, and Korea is an early-adopter market for social deduction (Among Us, GGD). Both favor our concept.
- Retention: these games flare and fade. Lethal Company is about 99% down, and Fall Guys cut lobby sizes. The ones that kept a long tail added **new places** (PEAK biomes, Human Fall Flat's 8 to 28 maps, 99 Nights' volcano) and **timed events and limited items** (Grow a Garden weekly items; Brainrot admin-abuse events).
- Implication for our game (progression and "feel progress", the user's direct ask):
  - **Two progress bars at all times:** the *run* (floor and altitude in this climb) and the *self* (persistent power tree, layer unlocks, skin collection). Grow a Garden and Brainrot show that constantly ticking passive growth ("number go up") is extremely sticky, even for simple games.
  - **Layers are the "new place" content drops.** Well, then Sky, then Space works as a live-ops roadmap. Each layer launch is an event, like the PEAK biome updates.
  - **Weekly limited doors and items** (the Grow a Garden pattern) and **"God's Whim" events** (the admin-abuse pattern): timed tower-wide events where the watchers or sponsors flood a floor with rare drops or weird rules.
  - **Daily seed and shared talk:** the PEAK daily map rotation, translated to "today's door layout".

#### Cross-cutting design brief for the tower game (synthesized from all sections)
1. **Coupled bodies:** every room has a shared-fate gimmick (tether, shared oxygen, fragile relic, linked controls). Physics and ragdoll are on by default.
2. **Blame and reveal:** a replay or "who did it" tag on every fail and every betrayal. Room exits end on a punchline.
3. **Betrayal is scoped, telegraphed and forgiven:** losses stay within the room or floor, never permanent. Victims become ghosts or sponsors who stay involved. The skin shop never sells sabotage.
4. **Short atoms, long arcs:** 2 to 6 minute rooms, then 15 to 25 minute floors, then days-long layers. The hub shop and minigames sit between floors.
5. **Bots are characters:** named rival climbers with personalities who bark, emote, betray visibly, fail funnily, and are replaced by humans mid-floor.
6. **Streamable by construction:** altitude on the HUD, readable goals, dramatic falls, chat-votes on doors, a one-link join and an auto clip buffer.
7. **Frictionless group entry:** a browser link with guest play, and a group bonus rather than a solo penalty.
8. **Live-ops as places and events:** new layers and doors, weekly limited items, daily seeds, and "God's Whim" events.
9. **Plan for abuse from day one:** server-authoritative actions, and anti-cheat and anti-DDoS measures, the failure mode that killed Dread Hunger and Deceit.

### Design conclusions

1. **Comedy = coupling + visible blame + low failure cost (Section 1)**: SUPPORTED qualitatively. Chained Together, PEAK, R.E.P.O. and Only Up! all fit. But this is a pattern read across hits, with survivorship bias and no counter-examples checked (coupled games that flopped). The Helldivers "consistency" support was a joke tweet, so it adds nothing.
2. **Betrayal must be scoped, rule-announced and mostly among friends; bots should be the main betrayers in public lobbies (Section 2)**: WEAK.
   - (a) The core "funnier with friends / fall from grace" evidence is one designer's anecdotal retrospective (Schell Games), not a study.
   - (b) The strongest engagement data in the file cuts the other way: Steal a Brainrot, built on stealing from strangers, reached 25.4M CCU, the largest number here. Toxic does not mean unpopular. The rule is justified as a wellbeing and brand guardrail, not as an engagement maximizer.
   - (c) Dread Hunger and Deceit died of DDoS, cheating and costs, not betrayal design.
   - (d) Bot betrayal lacks the human trust that the source says makes betrayal funny, so "bots as main betrayers" is untested and needs playtesting.
3. **Short atoms inside long arcs; every atom ends on a hook (Section 3)**: SUPPORTED, with a caveat. It fits Fall Guys, Among Us and Buckshot Roulette. But the Among Us match length comes from a low-authority site, and two of the biggest hits (a Lethal Company quota of about 40 min, a full PEAK climb) have long units. Short rooms are a choice, not a law the evidence proves.
4. **Streamer seeding, readable goal and loss, and near-zero price drive virality; browser zero-install is a "Content Warning-level" friction advantage (Sections 4 and 6)**: seeding and readability are SUPPORTED (Fall Guys codes, Among Us via Sodapoppin, Chained Together streamer groups, Only Up rage clips). **"Browser = Content Warning-level advantage" is WEAK.** Content Warning's spike came from being free *inside Steam* (store front page, 6.2M one-click claims, an existing Landfall audience, Steam charts). A browser game has zero install but lacks Steam's discovery, library and wishlist loop. The ordered "virality drivers by evidence strength" list is not actually ranked by any evidence.
5. **Long-tail retention comes from "new places" plus timed events and limited items (Section 6)**: SUPPORTED, and stronger than the notes claim. Grow a Garden's CCU records line up with named updates, PEAK's all-time CCU peak came in the Mesa update month (Aug 2025) and not at launch, and SteamCharts shows R.E.P.O. with a +164% month in Nov 2025 after content activity. This is the best-evidenced lever for the user's "feel progress / addicting" goal. The companion claim that constantly ticking passive growth is "extremely sticky" rests on Roblox games that also run heavy social and monetization loops, so treat it as plausible rather than proven.
6. **Bots as personality-driven characters, following Fall Guys' "hidden early bots tuned to lose" pattern (Section 5)**: WEAK as evidenced. The Fall Guys bot specifics are unverified here. No hit in the set gets its comedy from bots, and the genre definition itself says these games are unenjoyable alone. It is still a reasonable design bet for a 1 to 8 player game with bot fill, but it is untested, not a documented pattern.
