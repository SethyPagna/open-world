Scope note: research done Sept 2026. Namu Wiki, arca.live, noveling.dev, Medium and RIDI pages returned 403 or were blocked for direct fetch, so their content appears here only where a search-result snippet summarized it (marked "snippet"). ## Q1. Why status windows, stat gains, rank tiers, job changes, hidden classes, legendary items and level-ups feel so good, and which moments fans share

### Takeaway
The status window works because it turns effort into visible numbers right away and gives the hero information nobody else has. Korean scholarship ties it to meritocratic "effort = reward" fairness that real life does not deliver. English readers describe it as a "drip feed" of dopamine. The most shared fan moments are the ones readers can act out themselves (the Solo Leveling "Daily Quest" workout) and public reveals where the hero's rank is shown to others.

### Cited Findings
- Kim Jun-hyun (Sungshin Women's Univ. professor and working web-novel author) says status windows do two jobs. (1) They put the hero's growth in numbers so readers see progress at once, with no need to compare against other characters. (2) They give the hero exclusive information other characters lack, which removes reader worry about the hero being weak. — [Sungkyunkwan Univ. News, 2020](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)
- Kim also argues that readers buy one episode at a time, so each installment has to pay off on its own. That pushes authors toward first-person narration centered only on the hero, "mirroring gaming experiences where the player serves as narrative center." — [Sungkyunkwan Univ. News](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)
- Kim names *The World* (2003) as the first true Korean game-fantasy novel, so the genre is more than 20 years old. — [Sungkyunkwan Univ. News](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)
- Moon Eun-hye, "Genre Conventions of Game Fantasy Web Novels" (대중서사연구 30(3), 2024, pp.183-213). Heroes grind willingly because of "clear goals and explicit procedures" plus "joy from item acquisition and leveling up." Status windows, quests and levels create a "charm of happiness" that readers enjoy second-hand. The typical hero is a "sincere munchkin": overpowered, but also diligent and virtuous, so the reward feels earned. The serial structure is "Crisis-Resolution-Crisis." — [KCI abstract](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003134446)
- Korean commentary: status windows are a keyword for "growth" and for "breaking through the irrational structure of reality." Modern fantasy, hunter and professional genres "overlay another world onto reality like a layer" through the status window (snippet). — [thewiki.kr 상태창](https://thewiki.kr/w/%EC%83%81%ED%83%9C%EC%B0%BD); related academic note that the 4th-generation (web-novel) hero is "no different from reality" and chases goals readers know from their own lives (snippet) — [search summary of KCI/OAK papers](https://oak.go.kr/central/journallist/journaldetail.do?article_seq=23480)
- English view: stat screens appeal because "effort yields immediate, visible progress, unlike real life where the grind sometimes feels endless." Each stat increase is "proof the protagonist's effort is paying off," making meritocracy literal (work hard, gain XP, level up) (snippet of Stella& Medium essay "Why do so many Korean webnovels begin with status windows?"). — [Medium, Stella&](https://stelland.medium.com/why-do-so-many-korean-webnovels-begin-with-status-windows-8e03f40dc5c0)
- Travis Baldree (narrator/author): "There is a drip feed of moments of advancement and the dopamine hit that comes with that." Will Wight: "I hate stories wasting my time." Pirateaba: "Pure emotional storytelling without progression is stagnation... Unrestrained progression without emotion is apathetic." — [Reactor, "The Rise of LitRPG"](https://reactormag.com/the-rise-of-litrpg/)
- English-reader criticism: some see stat screens as superficial add-ons. Quality varies hugely ("90%" of works are weak), and AI-generated titles in 2026 make that worse. — [Reactor](https://reactormag.com/the-rise-of-litrpg/)
- Solo Leveling premise: the "weakest E-ranked hunter" is picked by "a mysterious program called The System" that gives him alone "the unique ability to level up." — [Wikipedia: Solo Leveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- Most-shared fan moment: the System's "Daily Quest" (100 push-ups, 100 sit-ups, 100 squats, 10 km run) became a viral TikTok fitness challenge, with multi-day "30 day" series by creators. — [TikTok example](https://www.tiktok.com/@michaelh_h2o/video/7332164626043604270); [TikTok discover page](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/solo-leveling-daily-quest-workout)
- Hero catchphrases become brands. The anime's second season is subtitled "Arise from the Shadow," and the Netmarble game is *Solo Leveling: Arise*. — [Wikipedia: Solo Leveling season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2); [Wikipedia: Solo Leveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- Reader list-makers file "system window" series together with regression, tower climbing and dungeon crawling as one taste cluster. The status window is also used to show *other characters'* hidden potential (for example, an F-rank with hidden A-rank "reawakening" potential) (snippet). — [MyAnimeList stack](https://myanimelist.net/stacks/58503)

### Inferences
- The pleasure is **legibility plus exclusivity**. The number goes up where the reader can see it, and only the hero (and the reader) knows why.
  - Game implication: give each player a private, diegetic "System" window, a HUD panel styled as an in-world message box, that shows personal quests, hidden-class progress and secret room clear conditions. In a social-deduction game, private information *is* the power fantasy. Our genre fusion is a real advantage here: the status window doubles as the Mafia/Among Us secret role card.
- The "sincere munchkin" point matters. Readers want power that looks earned by diligence.
  - Game implication: tie stat gains to visible player actions (rooms cleared, weird conditions met, teammates saved or betrayed), never to passive time. Show the cause in the popup, e.g. "[Skill acquired: Door Sense Lv.1, reason: opened 10 doors first]".
- The shared moments are either **re-enactable** (daily quest) or **public reveals** (rank shown to a crowd).
  - Game implication: build shareable "screenshot moments": a rank-appraisal ceremony at hub return, a full-screen "HIDDEN CLASS UNLOCKED" card with rarity odds ("0.3% of climbers"), and a system message quoting the exact odd deed that unlocked it. Add a one-click share or copy of that card.
- Tiers (F to SSS), job changes and legendary items are all **discrete step-ups on a continuous meter**.
  - Game implication: small numbers every minute (stats, EXP), a medium step every run (skill or item), a big step every few sessions (rank-up or job change ceremony). Tier letters should mean something to others (visible over heads in the hub), not only to the owner.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on which specific panels or scenes fans screenshot or meme most (beyond the viral Daily Quest). Namu Wiki meme pages were not fetchable.
- The Medium essay could be read only as a snippet; its full argument (Korean "spec" culture, competition) is unverified here.

## Q2. Sa-i-da (사이다) vs go-gu-ma (고구마), and how authors pace them

### Takeaway
Goguma ("sweet potato," the choking frustration of eating one dry) is any stretch where villains or circumstances hold the hero back. Saida ("cider") is the fizzy, refreshing payoff. Korean web fiction is built on saida, and readers are openly hostile to long goguma. Practitioners treat goguma only as a short setup to make the saida pop. I found no reliable figure for the "typical" length of goguma; practitioner advice points to resolving it within the same episode or a few episodes.

### Cited Findings
- Namu Wiki (via search snippet): goguma is when the hero is constrained by antagonists or circumstances, producing frustrating, tedious stretches (slow progress, drawn-out romantic tension, long hardship). Saida is fast, satisfying development likened to soda. Web novels are "fundamentally based on saida." Saida is reinforced by fast pacing, concise prose, excluding goguma and using weak antagonists. Readers prefer heroes with moral superiority and confidence, and when a story adds frustrating goguma "readers can respond with extreme hostility." — [Namu Wiki 고구마(클리셰)](https://namu.wiki/w/%EA%B3%A0%EA%B5%AC%EB%A7%88(%ED%81%B4%EB%A6%AC%EC%85%B0)); [Namu Wiki 사이다(클리셰)](https://namu.wiki/w/%EC%82%AC%EC%9D%B4%EB%8B%A4(%ED%81%B4%EB%A6%AC%EC%85%B0))
- Seoul Alien essay #181 frames goguma and saida as two repeating "algorithms." Web fiction is sorted by "minimal emotional codes" rather than genre, and the typical plot runs: hero gains a supernatural edge, beats pseudo-rivals, reaches goals. The essay calls this "anti-narrative," database-style consumption (Azuma Hiroki) and treats emotion as the currency. — [Seoul Alien Substack #181](https://seoulalien.substack.com/p/181)
- Moon Eun-hye (2024) describes the episodic engine as "Crisis-Resolution-Crisis," a short loop inside each episode. — [KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003134446)
- Korean publishers market whole lists as "no goguma" ("고구마 없이 술술 읽히는 사이다," readable with no goguma), which shows goguma-avoidance is a selling point. — [RIDI storage on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@ridi_storage/post/DKRFCSkR2ti/)
- NCSoft's reader-facing blog frames "munchkin" (overpowered hero) novels as "속이 뻥 뚫리는 사이다 쾌감," saida pleasure that clears your chest. — [NCSoft Weekly Buff Pick](https://about.ncsoft.com/news/article/weekly-buff-pick-95-210303)
- The cliffhanger "promissory note" rule sets the effective goguma budget: a crisis cut must be "converted to cash in the next episode's opening." Following a life-threatening cut with a flashback makes readers feel deceived. — [StoryBench 절단신공](https://storybench.kr/blog/%EC%A0%88%EB%8B%A8%EC%8B%A0%EA%B3%B5-%ED%9A%8C%EC%B0%A8-%EC%97%94%EB%94%A9-%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0)

### Inferences
- Korean readers put up with goguma only as a **wind-up** for a bigger saida. The implied rule of thumb is roughly one episode of pressure, then payoff at the start of the next. That is about 5,000 characters, or a few minutes of reading.
  - Game implication: in-game frustration (a bad door, a sabotage, getting trapped by a gimmick monster) must be followed within one room or one short sub-level by a guaranteed chance to hit back: revenge flag, comeback buff, "the System noticed your humiliation" quest.
- In multiplayer, goguma is often **caused by other humans**: betrayal, griefing, a carried teammate. Readers accept goguma because they trust the author to pay it off; players have no such author.
  - Game implication: design "author-guaranteed" payoffs. A betrayed player gets a hidden "Vengeance" quest, and betrayers are marked for a later reveal ceremony. Betrayal should feel like the setup of a saida, not a dead end.
- Saida works best with **weak antagonists and onlookers who doubted the hero**.
  - Game implication: AI rival bots are natural "extras." Script them to underestimate, mock and then be shocked ("W-what? An F-rank cleared 3E?"). That delivers the onlooker-reaction beat without making real players the humiliated party.

### Gaps
- No sourced statistic on how many episodes of goguma readers tolerate before dropping a series. An arca.live craft thread on "how to place goguma and saida" could not be fetched, and Namu pages were 403.
- No reader-comment sentiment data (for example, how often "고구마" appears in KakaoPage comments before drop-off).

## Q3. The underdog-to-overpowered arc (weakest hunter, F-rank, "trash" class)

### Takeaway
The arc starts the hero at the bottom of a public ranking (E-rank "weakest hunter," F-class, a "trash" job), gives him a private, unfair edge (the System, regression knowledge, a hidden class), then lets him climb past everyone who looked down on him. Its hold on Korean readers is linked to a feeling that real ladders are fixed, and to the "second chance" fantasy of regression/possession/reincarnation (회빙환), which in 2026 is described as a "classic," not a trend. Reader fatigue with it is openly acknowledged.

### Cited Findings
- Solo Leveling's hero starts as "the weakest E ranked hunter" and alone receives the System. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- Commercial proof of that arc:
  - Web novel on KakaoPage from July 25, 2016 to Dec 29, 2021.
  - Webtoon launched March 4, 2018, with over 650 million cumulative readership in Japan on Piccoma.
  - The anime won five categories at the 9th Crunchyroll Anime Awards (2025), including Anime of the Year.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Solo Leveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- Regression, possession and reincarnation (회빙환) are now called "not a trend but a classic." What they share is a hero who "has already experienced one life or one failure and makes a better choice." Repetition is producing reader fatigue, and the 2025-26 twist is being possessed into a *villain or extra* who dodges the original story's disasters. — [OhmyNews/Daum, April 2026](https://v.daum.net/v/20260415103745581); [Daum, Sept 2025](https://v.daum.net/v/20250915232911815)
- Regression's appeal in manhwa is described as "a second chance at life to make better decisions and overcome life's injustices" (snippet). — [GameGeekFusion regression list](https://gamegeekfusion.com/best-15-regression-manhwa-to-read-in-2025-ranked/)
- Moon Eun-hye: readers get "compensatory satisfaction" when a sincere, diligent hero triumphs through effort. — [KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003134446)
- Kim Jun-hyun: the status window's exclusive information "address[es] reader anxiety about protagonist vulnerability." Readers enjoy the underdog's position without the fear. — [Sungkyunkwan Univ. News](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)

### Inferences
- The arc has three parts: **public low rank + secret high ceiling + a witnessed reversal**. All three are social, because the rank has to be public for the reversal to be seen.
  - Game implication: at game start, publicly assign every player a low "Awakening Rank" (mostly F/E, randomized) and secretly give each one a hidden class seed or ceiling. The fun is the reveal of who was secretly the SSS.
- In a multiplayer game *everyone* wants to be Sung Jin-woo. Only one person can be "the only one who levels up."
  - Game implication: make exclusivity **per-player and orthogonal**, not a single ranking. Each player gets a unique hidden class with its own weird unlock condition (e.g., "Clear a room without touching the floor," "Be betrayed three times"). Everyone can be special in a different way. Use AI bots to fill the "everyone else is weak" role.
- The "extra/villain possession" trend fits our design well.
  - Game implication: frame players as *extras* in the Tower's story ("you were not chosen"), so every climb is an extra stealing the protagonist slot. This also justifies betrayal: the Tower has one protagonist seat.
- Fatigue is real in Korea. Novelty now comes from **twists on the formula** rather than the formula itself.
  - Game implication: our novelty should sit in the room gimmicks and odd clear conditions (the *Tutorial Is Too Hard* flavor), while the progression skeleton stays familiar.

### Gaps
- No survey data comparing Korean vs English readers' tolerance for the "trash class to SSS" arc. English sources discuss LitRPG broadly, not the Korean hunter form specifically.
- No sourced data on *Overgeared*, *Ranker's Return*, *The Player Who Can't Level Up*, *Return of the Disaster-Class Hero*, *Nano Machine* or *The Greatest Estate Developer* individually (search budget went to the genre-level questions).

## Q4. How serialization (chapter length, per-chapter cliffhanger, daily updates, free then paid, wait-or-pay) engineers compulsion

### Takeaway
The Korean paid web novel is a unit of about 5,000-5,500 characters priced around 100 won, released 5-7 times a week. The first ~25 episodes (one "volume") are free and must end on an unresolved hook so readers pay for episode 26. Every episode ends on a 절단신공 ("cut-off technique") cliffhanger that has to be paid off at once in the next one. KakaoPage's "wait-or-pay" model, launched in 2014, turned daily return visits into revenue and became the global standard. By 2025-26 the model is being challenged: Webtoon dropped Daily Pass, and Naver pushes all-free current works plus ads and subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- **Chapter unit:**
  - Munpia's minimum for a paid episode is 5,000 characters, and many paid markets price about 100 won per 5,000-5,500 characters (with spaces). A typical episode is 5,000-6,000 characters, published 5-7 times a week (snippet). — [dcinside 웹소설 갤](https://gall.dcinside.com/mgallery/board/view/?id=tgijjdd&no=1089381); [dcinside AI 웹소설 갤: "1권 = 25화"](https://gall.dcinside.com/mgallery/board/view/?id=aiwriter&no=1440)
  - A brunch craft guide confirms 5,000-5,500 characters with spaces as the paid standard at about 100 won per episode. It gives 4,800-5,500 as optimal and says to split episodes over 9,000 characters; 1,000-2,000-character episodes feel unsatisfying. — [brunch @qsza45](https://brunch.co.kr/@qsza45/11)
- **Free-to-paid wall:** 25 episodes free is typical, with conversion around episodes 40-50 in some cases. Twenty-five episodes equals one print volume. If an incident fully resolves at episode 25, "there is little reason to click episode 26," so the free block must end on open curiosity (snippet). — [dcinside AI 웹소설 갤](https://gall.dcinside.com/mgallery/board/view/?id=aiwriter&no=1440). Munpia specifically: roughly the first 25-30 chapters are free, then each costs 100 coins. — [Novel Updates Forum](https://www.novelupdatesforum.com/threads/how-does-munpia-work.13941/)
- **Cliffhanger craft (절단신공):**
  - Readers click "next" not because the episode was fun but because they "cannot bear to stop." The final three lines matter more than the preceding 5,000 characters.
  - Four cut types:
    - Crisis cut (danger; readers go numb if it is overused)
    - Twist cut (new information or betrayal; the strongest engagement)
    - Declaration cut (the hero states intent; pairs with saida)
    - Question cut (a mysterious voice or message; needs a planned payoff)
  - Rules: cut mid-event (at "opening the door, a voice speaks from darkness," not after victory, reward and return home). Decide the last line first. Vary cut strength across the arc to avoid fatigue.
  - "Cliffhangers are promissory notes": pay them off at the next episode's opening.
  - Source: [StoryBench](https://storybench.kr/blog/%EC%A0%88%EB%8B%A8%EC%8B%A0%EA%B3%B5-%ED%9A%8C%EC%B0%A8-%EC%97%94%EB%94%A9-%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0)
  - Korean craft sources state that the cut technique is "directly tied to 연독률 (read-through rate) and sales" (snippet). — [Readus 연재의 기술](https://read-us.co.kr/View.aspx?No=3035247); [brunch @blueear64](https://brunch.co.kr/@blueear64/17)
- **Wait-or-pay (기다리면 무료, "gidamu"):**
  - KakaoPage launched it in the second half of 2014. Readers unlock one episode free after waiting 12-24 hours or pay to read now. The logic is a free funnel that builds traffic, then converts the most absorbed readers to paying. Daily visits to collect the free episode raised loyalty, and readers who didn't want to wait started paying (snippet). — [happist.com](https://happist.com/573049/%EC%B9%B4%EC%B9%B4%EC%98%A4%ED%8E%98%EC%9D%B4%EC%A7%80-%EC%84%B1%EA%B3%B5%EC%9A%94%EC%9D%B8); [Issue & Insights, Mar/May 2026](https://www.issueinsight.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2660)
  - Transactions rose from $17M (2014) to $85.5M (2016). The model was adopted by Piccoma, Tencent Dongman, Tapas and KakaoPage Indonesia. — [KED Global](https://www.kedglobal.com/kakao_page_corp/brandedContent/brd0001)
  - Revenue split on a sale: 30% Google Play, 20% Kakao, 50% publisher. — [Wikipedia: KakaoPage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KakaoPage)
  - Hankyung headline (2018): the revenue model that made KakaoPage's sales "jump 100x" (full article 403). — [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2018122502591)
  - "Wait-Until-Free Plus" (2023) widened free reading to up to 10 works a day. — [Issue & Insights](https://www.issueinsight.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2660)
- **2025-26 shift away from wait gates:**
  - WEBTOON discontinued Daily Pass on May 29, 2025 (it allowed one free episode per day on completed series). It was replaced with watch-an-ad unlocks and whole-series coin unlocks, under the message "No more waiting until the next day to read the next episode." — [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-06-05/webtoon-service-ends-daily-pass-feature/.225053); [K-Comics Beat](https://kcomicsbeat.com/2025/05/29/no-youre-not-losing-it-webtoon-got-rid-of-daily-pass/)
  - Naver Webtoon releases most currently running works fully free, without gidamu. It reports about 170M MAU against KakaoPage's weaker user acquisition. Kakao's Story division revenue fell 5% YoY in Q4 2025; the article's figure "1.918 billion won" is likely a unit error for about 191.8 billion won and is unverified. Naver also launched ads and a premium subscription (the reported price of $49.99/month looks doubtful; verify before citing). — [Issue & Insights](https://www.issueinsight.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2660)
- **Payer ratio:** WEBTOON Entertainment Q4 2025 reported about 163.2M MAU and 7.5M monthly paying users, roughly 4.6% paying (my calculation). Other summaries say "~160M"; figures vary by source. — [WEBTOON IR Q4/FY2025](https://ir.webtoon.com/news-releases/news-release-details/webtoon-entertainment-inc-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-0); [allkpop on FY2025 results](https://www.allkpop.com/article/2026/03/webtoon-entertainment-posts-902-million-usd-operating-loss-in-2025-nears-2-billion-usd-annual-revenue-focuses-on-fandom-growth)
- **Consumer friction:** 197 web novel/webtoon consumer complaints were filed with Korea's 1372 center in 2023-2025 and rising; 40.6% concerned cancellation or penalties (snippet). — [Daum, Aug 2026](https://v.daum.net/v/20260807120130530)

### Inferences
- The compulsion stack has three layers at different time scales:
  - A small unit (5,000 characters, a few minutes)
  - A guaranteed unresolved ending per unit
  - A daily reason to return (new episode or free unlock)
  - Game implication: one room or sub-level should last about 3-6 minutes. It should end on a **cut**, never on a menu: the next lettered door is already glowing, a system message reads "[A hidden condition has been detected in Door 3E...]", or an ally's betrayal is revealed. The payoff arrives in the first seconds of the next room.
- The "25 free episodes = one volume that must *not* fully resolve" rule maps onto the first session.
  - Game implication: the first 20-30 minutes should deliver one complete mini-arc (first rank-up, first hidden-class hint, first betrayal) and end on an open hook, e.g. "Your hidden class is 12% unlocked" or a sealed door on the next floor with a teaser. Do not end on a clean "Victory! Return to hub" screen.
- Use StoryBench's cut types as a **room-end event table**: crisis (collapse timer), twist (a teammate's secret role exposed), declaration (a player's class awakening announced to all), question (a voice behind a door). Vary strength across a floor so it does not numb players.
- Wait-or-pay timers hurt a *multiplayer* game: friends cannot coordinate around per-player energy gates, and the webtoon industry itself moved away from wait gates in 2025-26.
  - Game implication: keep the "reason to return daily" (Daily Quest, as in Solo Leveling's viral meme, and a daily rotating featured door or floor) but **never gate play time**. Monetize with cosmetic skins only, which our skin shop already plans.
- Only about 4-5% of webtoon readers pay, so the model depends on a huge free audience.
  - Game implication: the free core loop must be fully satisfying on its own; paying should be about showing off (skins, rank-ceremony effects), not progress.

### Gaps
- No hard published retention or read-through curves (for example, % of episode-1 readers reaching episode 25 or 100). A Ruliweb post on 연독률 failed to load (ECONNRESET), and KOCCA's 2024/2025 comic/webtoon user surveys are published only as data-request files or book-format reports, not fetched.
- No confirmed share of KakaoPage revenue that comes from "pay to skip the wait."
- A search-tool summary claimed "Munpia service ended December 28, 2023." I could not verify this, it conflicts with Munpia's live homepage ([munpia.com](https://www.munpia.com/)), and it likely refers to a sub-service. Treat as unverified.
- The Wikipedia figure of ₩1.19T for Kakao Entertainment's 2025 revenue appeared only in a search snippet and seems low; not used.

## Q5. Which of these translate to game mechanics, and which do not translate well to a multiplayer game

### Takeaway
Several features carry over almost directly because they are already game-like: the status window, visible growth every few minutes, rank-appraisal ceremonies, rare hidden classes with weird unlock conditions, per-unit cliffhangers and a daily quest. What breaks in multiplayer is the genre's core premise that **one** person is exceptional and everyone else watches in shock, plus author-controlled goguma pacing. Evidence from *Solo Leveling: Arise* shows that IP-driven power fantasy draws huge launches but does not by itself keep players.

### Cited Findings
- *Solo Leveling: Arise* (Netmarble, May 8, 2024): 5M DAU and over $10.3M in the first 24 hours; passed $100M revenue within three months. — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/solo-leveling-arise-revenue-surpasses-usd100-million-in-3-months). 50M players and $139M in the first six months. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/solo-leveling-arise-hits-50m-players-and-139m-revenue-in-first-six-months/)
- Arise decay: an estimated $38M for all of 2025, falling from about $4-6M per month early in the year to about $2M per month from June (aggregator estimate). — [GachaRevenue / ennead](https://revenue.ennead.cc/games/solo-leveling-arise)
- The status window's two functions (quantified growth; exclusive information) are inherently single-viewpoint devices. Kim ties the genre's first-person, hero-centered narration to the per-episode purchase model and to games where "the player serves as narrative center." — [Sungkyunkwan Univ. News](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)
- Grinding stays voluntary when goals are clear, procedures explicit, and item and level rewards frequent. — [Moon Eun-hye 2024, KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003134446)
- The Daily Quest mechanic jumped from fiction into real-life habit loops (TikTok challenge). — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/solo-leveling-daily-quest-challenge)
- Webtoon removed its wait-a-day mechanic in 2025 in favor of instant access ("No more waiting until the next day"). — [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-06-05/webtoon-service-ends-daily-pass-feature/.225053)

### Inferences

**Translates well (use directly):**
- **Private System window**: personal quests, secret clear conditions, hidden-class meter. It also serves as the social-deduction role card.
- **Visible growth every 1-3 minutes**: EXP ticks, stat popups ("[STR +1]") with a satisfying sound, level-ups mid-room. The Baldree "drip feed."
- **Rank-appraisal ceremony** on returning to the hub: an animated letter-grade reveal (F, E, D, C, B, A, S, SS, SSS) in front of all players and bots, with bots reacting.
- **Hidden classes behind strange conditions**: this matches the "weird non-combat clear condition" rooms. The unlock message should quote the exact odd deed.
- **Legendary or unique items** with per-server firsts ("First in this Tower to obtain..."), announced globally in system-message style.
- **Per-room cliffhanger cuts** and the "promissory note" payoff at the next room's start.
- **Daily Quest**: one small, silly, re-enactable daily task that gives a streak and a title.
- **First session as the "free volume"**: a full arc plus an unresolved hook at about 25 minutes.

**Does not translate well (redesign):**
- **"Only I level up" exclusivity.** With 1-8 humans, one player's saida is seven others' goguma. Fix: orthogonal per-player specialness, AI bots as the weak "extras," and team-level saida moments (a whole-party rank-up).
- **Author-controlled goguma.** Readers trust the author to pay off suffering; players cannot trust other players. Fix: system-guaranteed revenge or comeback hooks after betrayal or sabotage, and short frustration windows (within one room).
- **Omniscient first-person narration.** A single-hero POV does not exist in a party. Fix: each player's System narrates *their* story privately; public events are shown to all as "world messages."
- **Endless escalation (SSS and beyond).** Unbounded power creep breaks cooperative and competitive balance and puzzle rooms. Fix: power resets or scales per layer, like our concentric world (Well, Sky, Space). Each new layer re-ranks you as "frog in a new well," so you feel weak again and get a fresh underdog arc. This mirrors how regression resets are used in the genre.
- **Wait-or-pay energy timers.** They break friend coordination, and the webtoon industry is abandoning them. Use daily hooks without gating play.
- **Reliance on IP hype.** The Arise decay shows launch spectacle fades. Long-term hold must come from social loops (betrayal stories, rivalries, hub minigames), not only power numbers.

### Gaps
- No sourced post-mortem or player-retention analysis explaining *why* Solo Leveling: Arise revenue fell (content drought, gacha fatigue, single-player focus?). The reasons above are my hypotheses.
- No research found on multiplayer games that implement Korean-style status windows specifically (for example, Korean MMO takes on Solo Leveling). A separate search on multiplayer progression-fantasy games is recommended.

### Design conclusions

1. **The private "System" window as a power fantasy (legibility + exclusivity) that doubles as the social-deduction role card.** SUPPORTED for the legibility/exclusivity mechanism (Kim, Moon). WEAK on the fusion. Kim says the exclusive information exists because readers *cannot tolerate the hero in crisis*. In social deduction, private information is what *creates* suspicion and vulnerability. So the role-card use works against the comfort function the source describes, and needs playtesting.
2. **Goguma must pay off fast (within one room), with system-guaranteed revenge hooks after betrayal.** SUPPORTED as a direction: StoryBench's "pay the note at the start of the next episode," plus publishers marketing "no goguma." WEAK on the specific budget. There is no reader-tolerance data, the Namu sources were snippets only, and "one episode ≈ one room" is an unsourced mapping.
3. **Per-room cliffhanger cuts, and a first session that works like the "free volume" ending on an open hook at about 25 minutes.** SUPPORTED on the principle of ending on an unresolved hook. WEAK on the numbers. "25 free episodes" is not a stable norm (conversion is often at ep. 40-50, and NovelPia uses 15). And 25 episodes is about 125k characters, hours of reading, so mapping it to 20-30 minutes is arbitrary.
4. **No wait-or-pay energy timers, because "the webtoon industry is abandoning them."** WEAK as argued:
   - Kakao's gidamu was literally modeled on a mobile-game energy timer (Anipang hearts) and lifted purchase conversion from 3-4% to 25%.
   - KakaoPage *expanded* gidamu in 2023 (Plus).
   - WEBTOON replaced Daily Pass with *ad-watching* and coin gates while keeping paid Fast Pass.
   - Naver's "current episodes free, pay to read ahead" model is long-standing, not a 2025-26 shift.

   The conclusion (don't gate co-op play time) can still hold on multiplayer-coordination grounds alone, but the industry-trend evidence does not support it.
5. **The Arise decay shows IP hype doesn't retain players, so retention must come from social loops.** WEAK:
   - The 2025 decay figures are unverified mobile-only aggregator estimates that exclude the PC/Steam version.
   - A post-launch revenue decline is the normal curve for gacha titles, and revenue is not retention.
   - No retention data or post-mortem was found.

   "Social loops matter for retention" may still be true, but this case does not demonstrate it.

Additional note on the "only ~4-5% pay" inference: the blended 4.6% hides a roughly 10x regional spread (about 15% in Korea vs about 1.5% in the rest of the world). For a global game, plan for payer rates near the low end.
