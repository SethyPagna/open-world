# Korean tower, tutorial, scenario and death-game web novels and manhwa: why readers get hooked, and what that means for a co-op/betrayal tower game

Scope: The Tutorial Is Too Hard (TTITH), Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint (ORV), Tower of God (ToG), SSS-Class Suicide/Revival Hunter, Second Life Ranker, Return to Player, The Regressor Instruction Manual, Pick Me Up! (Infinite Gacha), Dungeon Reset, Everyone Else is a Returnee, plus Solo Leveling as the popularity benchmark. Every section ends with what it means for our game: a Three.js browser game for 1-8 players with bots, lettered sub-level doors (1A, 1B, 1E...), non-combat clear conditions, powers, teaming and betrayal, hub minigames, a skin shop, and concentric world layers (Well -> Sky -> Space).

Research caveats: Namu Wiki, Novel Updates, Fandom (ORV/TTITH wikis), TV Tropes, Medium and one Anime-Planet review blocked automated fetches (403/402). Where I rely on the search-result snippet of a blocked page, I say "(snippet)". Details I know from background reading but could not re-verify this session are marked [unverified].

## 1. Recurring structural devices and how they create tension and payoff

### Takeaway
All of these works run on the same loop. An impersonal "System" announces a room, its rules, a clear condition, a time limit, a reward and a penalty. Then an audience (gods, constellations, dokkaebi broadcasters) watches and pays out for how the room was cleared, not just whether. Tension comes from rules that make selfish or brutal play the easy route. Payoff comes when the protagonist finds a clever, hidden or rule-bending route that the audience rewards.

### Cited Findings
**System, difficulty tiers and floors (TTITH)**
- TTITH has three difficulty levels: Normal, Hard and Hell. The hero, Lee Ho-jae, is the only person who picks Hell, and Hell's first floor holds "endgame threats in Hard mode." — [ChapterBrief review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review)
- Ho-jae is a 26-year-old former pro-gamer, and he is the only person who picks Hell. — [ChapterBrief](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review) (snippet via search)
- The Tutorial has 100 floors, with residential (safe) areas every 30 floors. Higher difficulties give greater rewards. — [TTITH Fandom wiki "The tutorial"/"Floors"](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/The_tutorial) (snippet)
- Gods watch each player's performance. They can add or subtract points, give extra rewards or skills, and scout players as apostles. — [TTITH Fandom wiki](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/The_tutorial) (snippet)
- Floor 6, "Undead Defense", is a stop-the-undead-before-reinforcements-arrive stage. Hell challengers call it the worst stage, and it has to be played solo because companions won't survive Hell. — [TTITH Fandom wiki "Floors"](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/Floors) (snippet)
- The story opens with the hero stuck on floor 60. He needs a co-op partner to attempt the higher floors, on a difficulty nobody wants. — [ScrollEpics](https://www.scrollepics.com/blog/the-tutorial-is-too-hard/) / [Novel Updates](https://www.novelupdates.com/series/the-tutorial-is-too-hard/) (snippet)
- A named Floor Manager (Kiri) runs Hell difficulty, and the companion Kirikiri "provides warmth without softening the premise." — [TTITH Fandom](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/Floors) (snippet); [ChapterBrief](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review)

**Scenarios, audience and sponsors (ORV)**
- ORV's scenarios are triggered by dokkaebi (Korean goblins). Participants earn coins and "stigmas" (skills) and gain constellation sponsors. — [Wikipedia: ORV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omniscient_Reader%27s_Viewpoint)
- Constellations watch humanity's apocalypse as entertainment and sponsor interesting survivors. This creates "a dual power structure (survival ranking + constellation patronage)." A physically modest survivor who entertains the audience can outgrow their base strength, while a brilliant but boring one struggles. — [ChapterBrief ORV review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- Kim Dokja's edge is finite foreknowledge: "His foreknowledge has a structural expiration date," because reality drifts away from the novel he read. — [ChapterBrief ORV review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- Scenarios force people to act out stories for the constellations' amusement. — [Wikipedia: ORV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omniscient_Reader%27s_Viewpoint) (snippet)

**Tests, betrayal and team battles (ToG)**
- ToG is built around climbing a Tower through "tests of strength, wit, and dexterity across multiple floors administered by Guardians." — [Wikipedia: Tower of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_God)
- The Workshop Battle is held every five years on the 30th floor. Teams fight through dangerous games for special prizes. — [Wikipedia: ToG season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_God_season_2) / [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/tower-of-god-season-2-episode-14-review/)
- The series' defining betrayal: Rachel pushes Bam off the Tower after he helped her climb, protected her and nursed her, and then she laughs about it. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/tower-of-god-unanswered-questions/); [Animehunch](https://animehunch.com/tower-of-god-why-did-rachel-betray-bam/)

**Death as a resource (SSS-Class)**
- The core skill of SSS-Class Suicide Hunter: "the skill copies the ability of whoever kills him," and then "rewinds time to before his death." In the review's words, "Every death … is a calculation, not a setback." — [ChapterBrief review](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)
- The Tower's ranked-hunter hierarchy makes "the targets legible," so readers always know who holds which skill. — [ChapterBrief review](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)

**Regression and inherited foreknowledge**
- Second Life Ranker: Yeon-woo finds his vanished twin's hidden diary. His brother was betrayed by his party while climbing the Tower (Obelisk), and Yeon-woo climbs using the diary. — [Korean Webtoons Wiki](https://koreanwebtoons.fandom.com/wiki/Second_Life_Ranker) (snippet)
- Return to Player: gods turned everyone on Earth into players of a sadistic game. Sehan Kim saw everyone die, then gets a second playthrough to "beat the gods at their own game." — [Korean Webtoons Wiki / Webtoon Wiki](https://webtoon.fandom.com/wiki/Return_to_Player) (snippet)
- The Regressor Instruction Manual: Lee Giyoung is stuck in a death dungeon with weak stats. He spots a regressor and plans to manipulate him in order to survive. — [Korean Webtoons Wiki](https://koreanwebtoons.fandom.com/wiki/Regressor_Instruction_Manual) (snippet)
- Namu Wiki calls regression the dominant device across the whole Korean web-novel market, because it "plausibly explain[s] the main character's munchkin and success story" in any genre. — [Namu Wiki: Regression/Korea](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%ED%9A%8C%EA%B7%80%EB%AC%BC/%ED%95%9C%EA%B5%AD) (snippet)

**Exploits and rule-bending**
- Dungeon Reset: the dungeon resets its traps for the next "users," but the reset doesn't apply to the protagonist. He survives through "outside-the-box thinking and various exploits." — [Korean Webtoons Wiki](https://koreanwebtoons.fandom.com/wiki/Dungeon_Reset) (snippet)
- Everyone Else is a Returnee: the protagonist is left alone on Earth when the rest of humanity vanishes, and he prepares for their return. — [Novel Updates](https://www.novelupdates.com/series/everyone-else-is-a-returnee/) (snippet)

**Gacha**
- Pick Me Up! (Infinite Gacha): the gamer Han Seojin (master name "Loki"), who never pulled a hero above 5 stars, wakes up inside the gacha game as a 1-star hero, Han Isratte, who must survive dungeons under another player's control. — [Pick Me Up! Wiki](https://pick-me-up.fandom.com/wiki/Han_Isratte) (snippet)

**Status windows as a cultural default**
- Korean web novels open with status windows because of the country's mix of gaming (PC-café) culture and social values. "Every stat increase and skill unlock is proof that the protagonist's sweat and blood are paying off." — [Stella&, Medium](https://stelland.medium.com/why-do-so-many-korean-webnovels-begin-with-status-windows-8e03f40dc5c0) (snippet)

### Inferences (device -> playable mechanic)
| Device (source) | Why it creates tension/payoff | Mechanic for our game |
|---|---|---|
| **System announcement / scenario window** (ORV, TTITH) | Cold, legalistic rules set up the dread. The reader parses the rules alongside the hero, and loopholes pay off. | Every sub-level opens with a full-screen "scenario card" (Category / Difficulty / Clear Condition / Time Limit / Reward / Failure Penalty), a stinger sound and a typewriter reveal. Hidden conditions flash as `[Hidden condition achieved]`. The System is the game's narrator voice. |
| **Difficulty choice at the door** (TTITH Normal/Hard/Hell) | Choosing pain earns identity ("the only one who chose Hell") and bigger rewards. | Each lettered door shows a difficulty pip and reward multiplier. Hell doors are solo-only or 2-player max (echoing TTITH floor 6, where companions can't survive Hell). The first party to clear a Hell door gets a server-wide announcement and a title. |
| **Audience / sponsors** (ORV constellations, TTITH gods) | Style gets rewarded, not just survival. Weak but entertaining players can catch up. | AI "Watchers" comment in a chat feed (`The Watcher 'Frog King of the Well' is amused by your betrayal`) and tip coins for clever clears, clutch saves, dramatic betrayals and comedy. Each player picks a sponsor at run start, and the sponsor biases which acts pay out. This doubles as rubber-banding: new players earn by being fun. |
| **Dead players become the audience** (inferred from ORV's watcher/performer split) | Social-deduction games lose eliminated players. ORV shows that watching and meddling is itself engaging. | Eliminated players become "Constellations" who spend a small coin pool to send limited "indirect messages" (a fixed phrase list), sponsor a living player, or trigger one minor room event. Nobody sits idle. |
| **Tests / Workshop Battle** (ToG) | Team competition for scarce prizes on a set calendar. | Every 5th floor is a "Workshop" event floor: teams compete in a structured game (crown-capture, position test) for one legendary reward. |
| **Betrayal** (ToG Rachel, Second Life Ranker party) | Betrayal by a trusted ally is the most emotionally charged beat in the genre. | Some clear conditions reward only N of M players ("Only 3 may exit"). There is always a costly co-op alternative too, so betrayal is a choice and never forced. |
| **Death -> power** (SSS-Class) | Failure turns into progress, so each death is "a calculation." | Dying to a gimmick monster grants a "fragment" of its gimmick (a temporary or run-long power). Death never feels like wasted time. |
| **Regression / foreknowledge** (ORV, RTP, Regressor Manual, Second Life Ranker) | Knowledge is power, but it expires. | A persistent Codex ("Reader's Notes") logs each sub-level's rules once seen, and repeat visits start with a hint. Sub-levels roll mutators, so the notes go stale. One hidden-role variant gives a single player the "Regressor" role: they see the next door's clear condition, and others try to identify, protect or exploit them (Regressor Instruction Manual in multiplayer form). |
| **Exploits / systemic rules** (Dungeon Reset) | Readers love protagonists who break the system. | Build rooms from systemic, combinable rules (physics, lighting, sound, monster behavior) so that unintended clears are possible. Reward them with a "Rule-Breaker" tag and Watcher tips. |
| **Diary / found foreknowledge** (Second Life Ranker) | Lore that is also a strategy guide. | Scattered "diary pages" from earlier climbers hint at hidden pieces on specific doors. They double as a collection meta-goal. |
| **Gacha** (Pick Me Up) | A 1-star underdog climbing is a strong fantasy. | Between floors, a "Summon" altar pulls power cards with star ratings and a pity counter, paid with earned currency only. Keep paid gacha out and money on skins, for trust in an 8-player social game. |
| **Residential floors every 30** (TTITH) | Pacing breather, social space, spending. | Hub towns at layer boundaries (Well / Sky / Space): shop, minigames, leaderboard wall, sponsor shrine. |

## 2. Pacing of progress: reward frequency, power-spike rhythm, cliffhangers

### Takeaway
Korean web novels are sold per episode (about ₩100 each), and every episode is written to end on a cliffhanger. Readers judge pacing on a "cider" (fizzy relief) vs "goguma" (sweet-potato frustration) scale. The formula is short frustration followed by an emphatic release. Readers punish long repetitive stretches (SSS's reset loop, ORV chapters ~80-120, TTITH's time skips and rushed ending) and reward arcs that change the setting or rules (SSS's Murim arc).

### Cited Findings
- Web novels are released as episodes that readers buy for about ₩100 each, and authors end episodes on "exciting moments and cliffhangers." — [Wikipedia: Web novels in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_novels_in_South_Korea)
- KakaoPage pioneered "Waiting for Free": one episode unlocks free after a wait. Naver Series runs a similar "Free for You" system. — [Wikipedia: Web novels in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_novels_in_South_Korea)
- The Korean web-novel market grew from ₩10B (2013) to ₩400B (2018) and passed ₩500B in 2019. — [Wikipedia: Web novels in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_novels_in_South_Korea)
- In a Korean survey, 71.5% use "cider" to mean words or actions that refreshingly resolve a frustrating situation, and 56.8% use "goguma" (sweet potato) for frustrating situations or people. From 2015 the sweet-potato meme was used for how plots unfold in web novels and comics. — [Seoul Economic Daily, Feb 2026](https://en.sedaily.com/culture/2026/02/06/goguma-means-frustration-cider-means-relief-korean-word)
- SSS-Class manhwa arc rhythm: the Foundation arc runs about 50 chapters and is "the tutorial stretch." Readers cite a "slow stretch" between seasons 1 and 2 where "the reset structure feels repetitive." The Murim arc is the community-consensus peak because "the setting change gives the mechanic a new problem to solve." The manhwa hit chapter 151 in early 2026 and went on hiatus. The novel is complete at about 400 chapters. — [ChapterBrief reading guide](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-revival-hunter-reading-guide)
- SSS criticism: "The skill-collection loop becomes predictable once the pattern is established: target identified, death engineered, skill acquired." — [ChapterBrief review](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)
- ORV's main criticism is mid-run pacing that thins around chapters 80-120. — [ChapterBrief ORV review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- TTITH is slow "by design," which frustrates readers who want "fast power scaling." Its tower-only setting limits variety. — [ChapterBrief TTITH review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review)
- TTITH criticisms: time skips "out of nowhere," and final 100 chapters that are "rushed, poorly executed," with plot threads resolved offscreen. — [Kawaiimo review](https://www.kawaiimo.com/en/review-the-tutorial-is-too-hard) / [Anime-Planet review](https://www.anime-planet.com/users/MangaXexperts/reviews/145513) (snippet). A MyAnimeList reader said the dual timeline made their "brains hurt." — [MAL: The Tutorial Is Too Tough](https://myanimelist.net/manga/146856/The_Tutorial_Is_Too_Tough)
- Structural calendar beats: ToG's Workshop Battle every 5 years on floor 30 ([Wikipedia: ToG S2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_God_season_2)); TTITH's residential areas every 30 floors ([TTITH Fandom](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/The_tutorial), snippet).
- Munpia "rewards the one-line hook more aggressively" than other platforms. Naver Series "leans toward long-run serial engines with clear escalation logic." — [klitreads, May 2026](https://klitreads.com/2026/05/15/korean-webnovel-trends-mid-may-2026/)

### Inferences (pacing -> game)
- **Three-tier reward rhythm, modeled on episode, arc and season:**
  - Micro, the episode: every room event pays something visible (coins, XP tick, stat +1, Watcher message), every 30-90 s.
  - Mid, the floor or arc: a power spike every floor (about 5-10 min). The player picks 1 of 3 skills or a class branch. A floor ends on a "next door" cliffhanger: the next floor's lettered doors show partial scenario cards ("1E: ??? — Only one may leave").
  - Macro, the season: a layer transition (Well -> Sky -> Space) roughly every 30-45 min of cumulative play. The transition changes the rules, not just the numbers (SSS's Murim arc lesson: new setting = new problem for old powers).
- **Cider/goguma budgeting.** Frustration segments (a gimmick room that kills you, a betrayal) must be short and paid off within the same session with a loud release: a boss clear, a revenge window, a sponsor jackpot. Never end a session on goguma. End on cider plus a teaser.
- **Anti-repetition rule.** SSS's and ORV's criticized stretches came from repeating one pattern. Rotate clear-condition types, don't escalate the same type (see the list in section 4). Do not let a player see the same gimmick family twice in a row.
- **Session-ending hooks.** "Waiting for Free" shows readers accept timed free unlocks. An ethical translation: daily "sealed door" events and a daily free Summon, never energy walls that block play in a party game.
- **One-line hooks for doors.** Munpia's hook culture suggests each sub-level needs a one-sentence premise readable at a glance ("The room where the loudest player dies").

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study of how often protagonists level up per chapter in these works. The rhythm above is inference from arc structures and reader complaints.
- No Korean source was reachable for chapter-length conventions (commonly ~5,000 Korean characters per episode [unverified]).

## 3. Scenes fans cite as most memorable, and the emotions they deliver

### Takeaway
The scenes fans remember fall into five emotional buckets:
- **Cleverness:** out-thinking a rule.
- **Dread and moral pressure:** a scenario that makes people turn on the weak.
- **Awe:** a monstrous competent rival shown through someone else's eyes.
- **Betrayal:** Rachel.
- **Earned companionship:** rivals who become partners.

In multiplayer, a social game can produce all five between real players.

### Cited Findings
- ORV's "Awesome" moments include Dokja trying to protect a grandmother in the subway scenario. He gives a speech to stop the other passengers from killing her (moral dread plus heroism). — [TV Tropes: Awesome/ORV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Awesome/OmniscientReadersViewpoint) (snippet)
- Yoo Joonghyuk's introduction, told through text Dokja reads: he single-handedly kills the whole of his subway cabin (awe and dread through a rival's competence). — [TV Tropes: Awesome/ORV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Awesome/OmniscientReadersViewpoint) (snippet)
- Heartwarming and character beats fans single out: Dokja decides not to use his reading skill on Yoo Joonghyuk because that "would reduce him to a character again." Dokja talks Yoo Joonghyuk out of regressing, and Yoo then refuses to regress (the companionship payoff). — [TV Tropes: Heartwarming/ORV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Heartwarming/OmniscientReadersViewpoint) (snippet)
- MyAnimeList reviewers call Dokja "cunning and manipulative," bringing "new and innovative ways to clear each scenario" rather than brute force. They add "The characters CARRY" and call the comedy "surprisingly top tier." — [MAL: ORV](https://myanimelist.net/manga/132214/Omniscient_Readers_Viewpoint)
- ORV's two leads each change "because of the other's presence." — [ChapterBrief ORV review](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- TTITH fans rank the floors with intelligent monsters highest: green goblins, red goblin monks, and a floor whose objective is to find the doppelganger. That last one is a social-deduction floor inside the tower. — [Kawaiimo](https://www.kawaiimo.com/en/review-the-tutorial-is-too-hard) / [Anime-Planet review](https://www.anime-planet.com/users/MangaXexperts/reviews/145513) (snippet)
- TTITH readers also prize "the psychological aspect of knowing some characters won't survive" and challenges that "become more political with open moral questions." — same review snippet
- ToG: fans turned hard against Rachel, one of the "most hated" characters, because she betrayed Bam after everything he did for her and laughed about it. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/tower-of-god-unanswered-questions/); [Hate Sink Wiki](https://hate-sink.fandom.com/wiki/Rachel_(Tower_of_God))

- The Flame Emperor arc develops "the series' most sustained emotional thread across multiple reset cycles." — [ChapterBrief SSS guide](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-revival-hunter-reading-guide)

| Emotion | Source scene | Engineered moment in our game |
|---|---|---|
| **Cleverness / vindication** | Dokja's loophole clears; Dungeon Reset exploits | Every sub-level has an obvious clear, a hidden clear and, where possible, a systemic exploit. The hidden clear pays 2-3x and triggers a Watcher chorus. |
| **Moral dread** | Subway: passengers turn on the grandmother | "Sacrifice" conditions ("The room opens when one player's light goes out"), with a hidden cooperative solution that needs everyone's trust. A bot seat can be the "grandmother" to protect or betray. |

## 4. What makes growth feel earned vs cheap

### Takeaway
Growth feels earned when it comes from the protagonist's own reading of the rules, from a visible cost (pain, death, sacrifice, risk), or from finite knowledge that could run out. It feels cheap when it becomes a predictable loop, arrives offscreen, or is generic "random MC levels up." Reviewers praise ORV and TTITH mainly for competence and cost, not raw power.

### Cited Findings
- **Earned by intellect:** ORV readers praise clears via cunning rather than force ("outsmart" rather than overpower). — [MAL: ORV](https://myanimelist.net/manga/132214/Omniscient_Readers_Viewpoint)
- **Earned by cost:** TTITH's Ho-jae treats difficulty "as data to process rather than an obstacle." The art shows "damage accumulation on Ho-Jae" rather than hiding injuries. — [ChapterBrief TTITH](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review)
- He is defined by "his capacity to endure suffering and his willingness to self-harm for skill development," becoming someone who treats "pain as a resource." — [Kawaiimo](https://www.kawaiimo.com/en/review-the-tutorial-is-too-hard) / [ChapterBrief](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/tutorial-is-too-hard-review) (snippet)
- **Earned by risk and scarcity:** ORV's foreknowledge "has a structural expiration date," which creates "sustained anxiety rather than compounding confidence." — [ChapterBrief ORV](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- **Earned by effort made visible:** "Every stat increase and skill unlock is proof that the protagonist's sweat and blood are paying off." — [Stella&, Medium](https://stelland.medium.com/why-do-so-many-korean-webnovels-begin-with-status-windows-8e03f40dc5c0) (snippet)
- **Cheap by predictability:** SSS's loop "becomes predictable once the pattern is established." — [ChapterBrief SSS](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)
- **Cheap by genericness:** a MAL critic dismissed TTITH as "Random MC gets transported to a random game and has to levelup... never seen that before." Another praised its "satisfying growth" and "3 dimensional side characters." — [MAL: TTITH](https://myanimelist.net/manga/146856/The_Tutorial_Is_Too_Tough)
- **Cheap by offscreen resolution:** TTITH's last 100 chapters are faulted for threads "resolved offscreen." — [Kawaiimo](https://www.kawaiimo.com/en/review-the-tutorial-is-too-hard) (snippet)
- **Underdog framing:** Pick Me Up starts its hero as a 1-star unit after a real-life history of bad pulls. That makes later power read as a comeback, not a handout. — [Pick Me Up! Wiki](https://pick-me-up.fandom.com/wiki/Han_Isratte) (snippet)

### Inferences (earned-growth rules for the game)
1. **Power comes from discovery first, RNG second.** The biggest upgrades (hidden classes or "hidden pieces") unlock by finding hidden clear conditions, never from shop pulls. RNG power (Summon altar) stays sidegrade-sized.
2. **Show the cost.** Let players take on voluntary burdens (Hell doors, "curse" contracts from sponsors, self-damage-for-skill altars like TTITH's pain-as-resource) that visibly mark their avatar: scars, cracked armor, glowing brands. A cosmetic proof of suffering is itself a status symbol, and the skin shop can sell only non-earned variants.
3. **Make knowledge perishable.** The Codex gives hints, but door mutators change one rule per visit, so veterans stay advantaged without being bored (ORV's expiring foreknowledge).
4. **Rotate the puzzle verb.** Clear-condition families: survive X seconds, protect Y, find the imposter, stay silent, never look at the monster, carry the light, make the monster laugh, trade items, vote someone out, reach consensus, lose on purpose, don't clear (wait out). No two consecutive rooms share a family.
5. **Never grant power offscreen.** Every stat gain is announced by the System window with a sound and a number (Korean readers' main pleasure signal). Level-ups get a freeze-frame "status window" moment even in co-op.
6. **Underdog start.** Everyone begins as "F-rank / 1-star" in the Well. Later layers let players look back down at earlier layers, a literal "frog leaves the well" visual payoff.

### Gaps
- No quantitative reader surveys on "earned vs cheap" growth were found. This synthesis rests on reviews and genre commentary.

## 5. Popularity data (views, ratings, adaptations, sales) and which elements resonate

### Takeaway
The visible-leveling "system" fantasy (Solo Leveling) is the commercial ceiling, at billions of views and nine-figure game revenue. Tower climbing (ToG) is the longest-lived IP. The elements that scale best are legible progression, a rival/ally ensemble, and a watching audience.

## 6. Device -> playable mechanic for a co-op/betrayal tower game (consolidated design implications)

### Takeaway
The genre's addictiveness is a machine with six parts:
1. A cold System voice that states the rules.
2. Rooms whose rules tempt selfishness.
3. Hidden clears that reward cleverness.
4. An audience that pays for style and drama.
5. Visible numeric growth after every beat.
6. Periodic setting shifts that make old powers face new problems.

A multiplayer game can do something the novels can't: the betrayer, the clever one and the audience are all real people.

### Cited Findings (anchor evidence already cited above)
- Audience patronage lets entertaining but weaker performers exceed their base strength. — [ChapterBrief ORV](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)
- Gods add or subtract points, scout apostles and give skills. — [TTITH Fandom](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/The_tutorial) (snippet)
- Difficulty tiers scale rewards, and Hell floor 6 must be played solo. — [TTITH Fandom](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/Floors) (snippet)
- Fan-favorite floors are those with intelligent monsters and a find-the-doppelganger objective. — [Kawaiimo](https://www.kawaiimo.com/en/review-the-tutorial-is-too-hard) (snippet)
- Death that copies the killer's ability turns failure into a calculation. — [ChapterBrief SSS](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)
- The setting shift in the Murim arc is SSS's fan-consensus peak. — [ChapterBrief SSS guide](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-revival-hunter-reading-guide)
- Betrayal is the fastest way to polarize and engage a fanbase (Rachel). — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/tower-of-god-unanswered-questions/)

### Inferences: a concrete mechanic set
**A. Presentation**
- **The System voice.** Full-screen scenario cards, notification chimes, bracketed text (`[Main Scenario #3 has begun]`, `[You have acquired a Hidden Piece]`, `[Constellation 'Well-Watching Frog' sponsors you 300 coins]`). This UI is the genre's signature and costs little to build.
- **Status window moments.** Pause-on-level-up stat readouts, even mid-co-op, shown to other players as a floating banner.
- **Watcher chat feed.** A scrolling side feed of AI sponsor reactions, and the channel where betrayals get amplified ("The Constellation 'Lover of Tragedy' is delighted").

**B. Room design (sub-level doors 1A-1Z)**
- Each door has: a one-line hook, a difficulty (Normal/Hard/Hell), a player cap, a clear-condition family, a hidden clear, a failure penalty and a time limit.
- Include "Doppelganger" and "Only N may leave" doors on every floor, so social deduction happens inside the tower, not only in a separate mode.
- Rooms where parties from different doors converge (the user's "meet inside the same sub-level"). These work like ToG's Workshop Battle: temporary alliances against other teams for one prize.

**C. Progression**
- **Micro** (every 30-90 s): coins, XP ticks, Watcher tips.
- **Mid** (every floor): pick 1 of 3 skills, or a hidden class if a hidden piece was found.
- **Macro** (every layer): new world rules, a hub town and a visual "leaving the well" transition.
- **Meta**: a Codex of rules and hidden pieces, sponsor affinity levels, titles, and cosmetic scars earned from Hell clears. Sponsors can "scout" high performers as apostles, a prestige unlock (TTITH).
- **Failure**: death yields a copied gimmick fragment (SSS), so every run adds something.

**D. Social / betrayal**
- Betrayal is legible and consequential. Exits, keys and lights can be stolen. Betrayers get an infamy title and a visible bounty, and Watchers tip betrayal but others remember it.
- Bonded duos earn shared skills, which makes betrayal costly and dramatic (ORV's Dokja-Joonghyuk bond; Rachel's betrayal of Bam).
- Dead or eliminated players become Constellations with meddling powers, which keeps 8-player lobbies engaged.
- **Hidden role "Regressor"** (Regressor Instruction Manual, RTP): one player knows the next room's rules. Protecting, exploiting or unmasking them is a meta-game.

**E. Bots**
- Give bot seats personalities drawn from genre archetypes: the cold regressor, the loyal tank, the schemer (a Rachel type), the helpless civilian to protect. They then generate the moral-dread and betrayal scenes even in small lobbies.

**F. Monetization fit**
- Keep to cosmetic skins as planned. Offer an optional free daily Summon or sealed-door event for a "come back tomorrow" hook, like KakaoPage's Waiting for Free. Never sell power, because it would break the "earned growth" rule readers care about.

### Gaps
- None of these mechanics is validated by playtest data. They are design translations of narrative devices and should be prototyped and measured (session length, return rate, betrayal frequency).
- No sources were found on existing games that implement ORV-style audience sponsorship in multiplayer, so no comparative evidence on how well it works.

- **Floor 3** has an intended easy route on the ground, hidden in fog, and a harder aerial route. The manager tells Ho-jae, "you cleared it in a weird way!" This is canonical support for the "hidden clear / unintended clear" door design.
- **Floor 61** says the more companions you sacrifice to one ruler, the weaker the other boss gets. This is a canonical "betray allies for an easier clear" condition.
- **Floor 35** reverts you to your weakest body to re-walk 34 earlier gates "in a different way," which is a regression floor.
- Korean Wikipedia credits TTITH (2016) with pioneering the gods-as-observers ("constellation") trend. That trend predates ORV (2018).

### Design conclusions

- **D1. Three-tier reward rhythm** (micro every 30-90 s, a spike every floor, a rule-changing layer shift every 30-45 min): **WEAK as specified, SUPPORTED in principle.** Two sourced points back the idea that a layer shift must change rules, not numbers: SSS's Murim arc is the consensus peak, and ORV readers complain about repetition in chapters 80-120. But every interval number is invented, and the episode-to-30-second analogy has no evidence behind it. The notes themselves say no pacing data exists. Treat the intervals as playtest starting values, not findings.
- **D2. AI Watchers/sponsors paying for style, doubling as rubber-banding:** **SUPPORTED as genre fit, WEAK as proven mechanic.** The device is central to both TTITH (which pioneered it) and ORV. However, the claim that "ORV's audience layer resonates more than TTITH" rests on MAL membership of the *manhwa* adaptations (177,982 vs 14,499). That gap is confounded by adaptation quality, Naver vs Kakao distribution, and film/anime marketing, and TTITH also has the audience device. There is no evidence from multiplayer games. The risk: paying for "drama" can reward griefing and betrayal spam unless the payout is capped or tied to the room's rules.
- **D3. Death yields a gimmick fragment (SSS), so no death is wasted:** **WEAK.** It is faithful to the source, but the notes' own source says SSS's loop "becomes predictable once the pattern is established." In a betrayal game, death rewards can also encourage suicide-farming and blunt the stakes of being betrayed. It needs caps, diminishing returns, or fragments that only matter in *different* rooms.
- **D4. Betrayal as an optional, costly, legible choice (N-of-M exits, stealable keys, infamy title):** **SUPPORTED.** Rachel's betrayal is the genre's defining fan-polarizing beat. TTITH floor 61 is a canonical sacrifice-your-companions clear, and ORV's first scenario is a room whose rule pushes people to kill the weak but which has a clever non-lethal clear (the insects). One caveat: remove "laughs about it" from the rationale because it is unsourced, and do not name the in-game infamy title "Rachel," which is another IP's character name. Use an original title.
- **D5. "Regression is still the #1 hook in 2026," so use a roguelite regression framing:** **WEAK evidence, fine as flavor.** The "#1" rests on one small blog and a Namu snippet, neither re-verified. Regression's prevalence is plausible (titles like *Absolute Regression* and *Max-Level Player's 100th Regression*), but nothing here measures it as the top hook. The design choice doesn't depend on it, because a regression framing is justified by meta-progression needs alone.
