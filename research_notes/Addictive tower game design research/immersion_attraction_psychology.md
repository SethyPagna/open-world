# Immersion, First-Sight Attraction, Engagement Psychology and Ethical Monetization (for a 1-8 player co-op/party/social-deduction "tower of lettered doors" browser game)

Scope note: this file covers (1) immersion and game feel, (2) character/art attractiveness and store-page first impressions, (3) engagement and progress psychology, (4) retention and ethical monetization, and (5) onboarding and first-session red flags. Each section ends with "Inferences" that apply the findings to our game (Three.js, 1-8 players with AI rival bots, lettered doors 1A/1B/1E..., gimmick monsters with odd clear conditions, powers, team-ups, sabotage/betrayal, hub minigames, cosmetic shop, concentric "frog in a well" layers Well -> Sky -> Space). Source quality is flagged where a claim comes from an aggregator, forum or blog rather than a primary source.

## Q1. Which concrete techniques make players FEEL progress every few minutes, even when they fail a run?

### Takeaway
Players stay when the game feeds three needs (competence, autonomy, relatedness) and when every run, including a failed one, visibly moves several meters: narrative, knowledge, collection and currency. Head-start progress (pre-filled bars), bars that speed up near the goal, and a visibly "interrupted" next task are the best-evidenced cheap levers; stat-gated meta-progression is the most criticized version.

### Cited Findings
- Self-determination theory (SDT) in games: four studies found that perceived in-game autonomy and competence are associated with enjoyment, game preference and changes in well-being before vs after play; autonomy, competence and relatedness independently predict enjoyment and future play. Competence and autonomy were also related to intuitive controls and to the sense of presence/immersion. — [Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006, Motivation and Emotion (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8); [PDF on selfdeterminationtheory.org](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf)
- Follow-up model of video game engagement by the same group (Przybylski, Rigby, Ryan 2010) extends the need-satisfaction account. — [Review of General Psychology PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2010_PrzybylskiRigbyRyan_ROGP.pdf) (not read in full; cited for existence)
- Quantic Foundry's Gamer Motivation Model: 12 motivations from factor analysis of survey data from 466,000+ gamers, grouped in 6 pairs (Action: Destruction/Excitement; Social: Competition/Community; Mastery: Challenge/Strategy; Achievement: Completion/Power; Immersion: Fantasy/Story; Creativity: Design/Discovery); the same high-level structure appears across regions in 3 clusters. — [Quantic Foundry model page](https://quanticfoundry.com/gamer-motivation-model/); [GDC Vault: Deep Dive into the 12 Motivations](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025742/a-deep-dive-into-the); [Reference PDF](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Gamer-Motivation-Model-Reference.pdf)
- Endowed progress effect (Nunes & Drèze 2006): car-wash loyalty cards needing 8 paid washes; the card with 10 slots and 2 pre-stamped was completed by 34% vs 19% for an 8-slot empty card with the same real requirement, and completed faster. — [Learning Loop summary](https://learningloop.io/plays/psychology/endowed-progress-effect); [Silicon Canals summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-car-wash-loyalty-cards-endowed-progress/) (secondary summaries of the Journal of Consumer Research paper; primary not fetched)
- Goal-gradient (Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, Journal of Marketing Research): café customers bought coffee more often as they neared the free coffee; a 12-stamp card with 2 "bonus" stamps was completed faster than a plain 10-stamp card (median 10 vs 15 days). — [Columbia Business School summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration); [Paper PDF (UChicago)](https://home.uchicago.edu/ourminsky/Goal-Gradient_Illusionary_Goal_Progress.pdf)
- Zeigarnik effect, corrected: a 2025 meta-analysis (Ghibellini & Meier, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications) found that, excluding Zeigarnik's 1927 data, the memory advantage for interrupted tasks essentially disappears (ratio about 0.99), but the Ovsiankina effect (spontaneously resuming interrupted tasks) replicates well, with a weighted resumption rate of about two-thirds. — [Yu-kai Chou summary of the meta-analysis](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/zeigarnik-effect-incomplete-tasks-memory-tension/) (secondary; primary paper not fetched); background on Ovsiankina — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovsiankina_effect)
- Quest logs and "one more turn": games keep a list of unfinished tasks, and finishing one immediately offers another with a bigger reward; "one more turn" in Civilization is usually in service of completing a structure/tech/conquest. — [Psychology of Games (Jamie Madigan): Zeigarnik Effect and Quest Logs](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2013/03/the-zeigarnik-effect-and-quest-logs/); [LinkedIn essay](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/zeigarnik-effect-psychological-principle-makes-games-felipe-carreiro) (opinion)
- Hades made failure progress: team mantras "in death, there is progress" and "every run counts"; each death advances a persistent story and lets you invest in upgrades; the team avoided a traditional easy mode but let struggling players still move the story forward. — [Game Developer: How Supergiant weaves narrative rewards into Hades' cycle of perpetual death](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death); [Inlander interview with Greg Kasavin](https://www.inlander.com/culture/hades-writer-greg-kasavin-on-how-he-made-video-game-deaths-drive-a-feel-good-story-22725237); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-hades-made-a-genre-known-for-being-impossibly-hard-accessible/)
- Hades uses several currencies, each tied to a different type of progression, and once most upgrades are maxed players switch to the Heat system to add difficulty instead. — [DMS 462 student analysis](https://dms462fall2020.wordpress.com/2020/12/06/meta-is-etymologically-greek-right-meta-progression-in-hades/) (student blog; consistent with the game)
- Backlash against meta-progression: players complain about roguelites "designed around unlock to feel good" and about stat-based meta-progression that makes early runs feel artificially gated. — [Hades II Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145350/discussions/0/4358999171576511867/?ctp=2); [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/im-starting-to-feel-that-stat-based-meta-progression-is-starting-to-ruin-roguelites-generally-speaking.1509337/) (forum opinion)
- Design guidance: meta-progression should make a failed run feel like progress without trivializing the skill core. — [Bugnet blog](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-roguelite-meta-progression) (practitioner blog)

### Inferences (what this implies for our tower game)
- Make every run fill at least 4 separate meters shown on a post-run "results ceremony" screen, animated one after another: (1) Tower Codex, a door-letter grid per floor (1A-1Z) that fills when you discover a door, even if you die inside it (Completion); (2) Monster/Gimmick Bestiary, where learning a gimmick's odd clear condition unlocks its page even on a failed attempt (knowledge as progress: Discovery/Mastery); (3) account XP / season pass; (4) a small premium-free cosmetic currency. A run that ends in 90 seconds still fills 2-3 meters.
- Endowed progress: the tutorial should pre-stamp the first Codex page ("Floor 1: 2/10 doors found") and the first pass tier. Show the bar as 20% full on minute one, not empty.
- Goal-gradient: put the nearest-to-completion bar last on the results screen, and end sessions with "1 door left to complete Floor 2's page" style prompts. Speed up visual fill (bigger chunks, sparkle, sound pitch rising) as a bar nears full.
- Ovsiankina (resumption), not "memory": end sessions on a visibly interrupted task, such as a sealed door 2E you glimpsed but could not open, a half-solved puzzle whose state is saved, or a rival bot who "went ahead" to floor 3. Show it on the main menu next launch ("Continue: the door you left open").
- Hades pattern for betrayal and death: death returns you to the hub, where NPCs and rival bots comment on what happened ("You trusted Kim in 3C again?"). Every death unlocks a line of dialogue or a lore scrap about the tower's "tutorial" administrators. This turns the web-novel premise (the tutorial that is too hard, regressing/returning after death) into narrative progress.
- Prefer horizontal progression (new doors, powers that change options, lore, cosmetics, new hub minigames) over raw stat boosts; add an opt-in "Hell difficulty" contract system like Hades' Heat once players master floors, echoing the novel's Hell-difficulty tutorial.
- SDT mapping: autonomy = choosing which lettered door, which power, and whether to help or betray; competence = readable feedback on every puzzle step; relatedness = shared rooms, team-ups, rivalries. Betrayal must feel like play, not griefing, or it attacks relatedness (see Q5).
- Target cadence: a reward "pop" every 30-60 s (room object solved, coin, codex stamp), a sub-level clear every 3-6 min, a floor clear or layer reveal every 10-15 min, a pass tier every 1-2 sessions.

### Gaps
- No primary experimental source fetched on variable-ratio reward schedules in games (Skinner-style schedules are commonly cited, but I found no game-specific study in this pass). Treat "random rewards are more compelling" as plausible but unverified here, and note its ethical risk (see Q4).
- The 2025 Zeigarnik meta-analysis was read only via a secondary summary.
- No published data on the optimal reward interval (for example "every 3 minutes") in party/co-op games; the cadence above is an inference.

## Q2. Which character designs and art styles perform best for co-op party games, and why (silhouette, customization canvas, expressiveness)?

### Takeaway
Winning party-game mascots are simple, rounded, toy-like blobs with a big, clean silhouette, few limbs and little or no facial detail. They are funny when they fail, readable at small on-screen sizes, and serve as a large blank canvas for hats, prints and full "mascot suits." On store pages, top sellers use a clear title plus one focal point readable at thumbnail size.

### Cited Findings
- Fall Guys bean shape came from "weeks of testing": a humanoid form felt too aggressive, a circle too simple; the bean "was instantly loveable and funny when it fell." — [ESPN: Meet the minds behind the Fall Guys costumes](https://www.espn.com/gaming/story/_/id/29734365/meet-minds-fall-guys-costumes)
- Fall Guys design was inspired by a chunky yeti-shaped vinyl toy; costumes are designed to look "both ridiculous when they win and hilarious when they fail"; because Fall Guys are small on screen and "little details can get lost," designers zoom out to test what matters; colors are pushed ("even our pastels are pushed to the limits"); designs should read even when only partly visible. — [PlayStation Blog: Creating the Character Designs of Fall Guys (2020)](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/05/25/creating-the-character-designs-of-fall-guys-out-on-ps4-this-summer/)
- Fall Guys are built to be customized: vinyl-toy influence, big prints and silly mascot suits; "the Bean is your canvas." — [GodisaGeek on the creation/customization video](https://www.godisageek.com/2020/05/fall-guys-creation-and-customisation/)
- Among Us crewmate (by InnerSloth co-founder Marcus Bromander) was kept simple because it "allows nearly anyone to be able to draw the character and express themselves through it"; the design settled almost immediately (only one concept sketch). — [Screen Rant: concept art](https://screenrant.com/among-us-concept-art-crewmate-design/); [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/among-us-concept-art-early-look-crewmate-design/); customization is hats, skins, pets — [Among Us Wiki: Cosmetics](https://among-us.fandom.com/wiki/Cosmetics)
- Commentary: the crewmate's lack of facial expression and limbs adds mystery in a deduction game, because you cannot read who is lying from the character. — [AppleGazette guide](https://www.applegazette.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-every-among-us-character-colors-roles-and-secrets/) (fan/aggregator opinion, not a developer quote)
- PEAK (Aggro Crab + Landfall, released 16 June 2025): first-person co-op climbing for up to 4 players on a procedurally varied mountain; sold over 100,000 copies in its first 24 hours. — [Wikipedia: Peak (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game)). Its scouts (mushroom hats, badge sashes) became a fan-art and 3D-print meme around its "struggle-core" co-op identity. — [Shapes fandom page](https://shapes.inc/fandom/peak); [Cults3D fan models](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/art/peak-game-scout-character-on-luggage-3d-model-stl-aggro-crab-landfall-fan-a) (fan sources; evidence of meme-ability, not of design intent)
- Steam capsule practice: the capsule is the game's first impression; aim for one mood, one character, one focal point, with the genre obvious in under one second; a face is the fastest genre signal for character-driven games; it must read at the smallest size (120x45 px is cited). — [presskit.gg capsule art guide (2026)](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/steam-capsule-art-guide); [presskit.gg page optimization guide](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/steam-page-optimization-guide) (practitioner guides)
- Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game): have a professional artist make the capsule before spending on ads; show at least three unique environments in screenshots so the game does not look like an asset flip; trailers should show in-game footage from the opening seconds, not CGI. — [Game World Observer summary of Zukowski guide (Mar 2025)](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/03/11/steam-page-launch-guide-wishlists-zukowski); [Zukowski, 60 Game Marketing Mistakes (PDF)](https://howtomarketagame.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Zukowski_60MistakesEbookV1.pdf)
- Capsule data from 98 top sellers: 99% show prominent title text; 20% show a readable face; 41% show any figure or creature; 25% are logo-only; 45% use dark backgrounds (median luminance 82/255); 69% are clearly readable at 120 px, and most illegible ones are established franchises relying on brand recognition. — [Steam Page Analyzer: Capsule data from 98 top sellers](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-capsule-trends-data) (third-party analysis)

### Inferences (what this implies for our tower game)
- Adopt a "toy mascot" climber: a rounded capsule/bean or stubby adventurer body, 1.5-2 heads tall, stubby limbs, large uninterrupted torso and head surfaces for prints, and one strong color block. Use a minimal face (two dot eyes, optional mouth decal) so emotion comes from body animation (wobble, flop, squash) and so deceivers cannot be read from faces, as with the Among Us crewmate.
- Test every skin at the real in-game camera distance with 8 players on screen. If a skin does not read as one clean shape plus one color at 40-60 px tall, simplify it.
- Make failure funny: physics-driven falls, comic ragdoll when a gimmick monster "disqualifies" you, and a squash-and-stretch pop when you respawn. Failing in front of friends should produce laughter and clips, not frustration.
- Give each world layer a signature palette (Well: mossy green/stone; Sky: pastel blue/white cloud; Space: deep violet/neon), which also satisfies Zukowski's "three unique environments" rule in screenshots.
- Capsule/thumbnail: one mascot climber at a lettered door (door letter glowing, a hint of sky or space beyond), a big legible logo, and a dark or mid-tone background so the colorful mascot pops. The first 3 seconds of the trailer: 8 mascots burst through a door into a weird room with a monster and a visible objective.
- Anime-style "hero" characters (Genshin, Solo Leveling) are better used as unlockable NPCs, tower administrators or bosses than as the player avatar, because detailed humanoid designs lose readability at party-game scale and fight the "blank canvas" cosmetic model. This is an inference; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- No developer-sourced material found on why PEAK's scouts appeal, or on Genshin Impact / Solo Leveling character appeal (these would need Korean/Japanese/Chinese design interviews or fan research not covered in this pass).
- No A/B data found comparing capsule styles on click-through for party games specifically.

## Q3. What does research say about immersion (presence), and which techniques create it cheaply in stylized 3D?

### Takeaway
In research, presence is less about photorealism than about intuitive control, active agency and perspective: SDT work links presence to intuitive controls and need satisfaction, and first-person, active play raises spatial presence. In practice, the cheapest immersion multiplier is "juice" (responsive animation, hit-stop, shake, particles, sound), which experiments have shown can transform a dull prototype.

### Cited Findings
- Presence/immersion was related to intuitive controls and to competence/autonomy satisfaction. — [Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8)
- Spatial presence is defined as media content being perceived as "real" in the sense of feeling located in the mediated environment. In an fMRI/VR shooting study, first-person perspective produced higher spatial presence than third-person, and active play produced higher presence than passive watching. — [Perspective and agency during video gaming influences spatial presence (PMC, 2012)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3476392/); related [Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2017 on 1PP vs 3PP](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2017.00033/full)
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of how game design choices affect presence exists (Applied Ergonomics, 2022). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687022002599) (abstract could not be fetched, HTTP 403; effect sizes not verified)
- Weibel & Wissmath examined immersion via spatial presence and flow as related but distinct constructs. — [Semantic Scholar: Immersion in Computer Games: The Role of Spatial Presence and Flow](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Immersion-in-Computer-Games:-The-Role-of-Spatial-Weibel-Wissmath/799d8b5c846b442bda92d516f679f42d64cedc4c) (title/abstract level only)
- Spatial audio has been studied as a contributor to presence in virtual environments. — [Aalborg University report on spatial audio, immersion and presence](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/213012770/151031_Rapport.pdf); [ResearchGate: spatial sound and presence](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261523769_Integration_of_spatial_sound_in_immersive_virtual_environments_an_experimental_study_on_effects_of_spatial_sound_on_presence) (not read in full)
- "Juice It or Lose It" (Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, 2012) showed live that a plain Breakout clone becomes compelling by adding tweening, squash, particles, screen shake, flashes and sound, without changing rules. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose); [Arzola's recap](https://devblog.heisarzola.com/gdcr-juice-it-or-lose-it/)
- "The Art of Screenshake" (Jan Willem Nijman, Vlambeer, 2013) applies about 30 tweaks to animation and sound in about 25 minutes to a boring platform shooter, including hit-stop (freezing a few frames on hits, kills and explosions) and camera shake. — [Pepwuper writeup](https://pepwuper.com/jan-willem-nijman-co-founder-of-vlambeer-on-the-art-of-screenshake/); [Mary Rose Cook notes](http://notebook.maryrosecook.com/Theartofscreenshake,JanWillemNijman.html); [talk video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U)
- Web-game specific: squash-and-stretch, shake and similar juice techniques are practical in browser games. — [Game feel on the web: squash, shake, and the art of juice](https://valdemird.com/blog/game-feel-on-the-web/) (practitioner blog)

### Inferences (what this implies for our tower game)
- Spend on responsiveness before fidelity: sub-100 ms input response, coyote time and jump buffering for climbing, generous grab/interaction radii, clear interact prompts. This is the presence lever the research supports and it is cheap in Three.js.
- Juice checklist per interaction: door open = anticipation squash, 3-6 frame hit-stop, dust burst, camera nudge, bass "thunk," letter glowing then flipping; puzzle piece placed = snap tween plus rising-pitch chime; monster gimmick triggered = screen tint and slow-mo 0.2 s; room clear = confetti burst, all players' mascots doing an auto-cheer, loot flying to the HUD (the "reward travels to the bar" effect makes progress feel physical).
- Perspective trade-off: third-person lets players see and show off cosmetics (drives purchases and social recognition); first-person raises spatial presence (PEAK uses it for co-op climbing). Suggested hybrid: third-person default, with scripted first-person or close camera for awe and scale moments (peeking over the well's rim, the first view of the sky layer, looking down from a sky bridge).
- Sense of scale for the "frog in a well" fantasy: make the layer transitions ceremonial. Pull the camera back from the well floor to reveal how small it was; swell the music; add fog layers and parallax skyboxes; drop in huge silhouettes (sky whales, space structures) at a distance. Seeing the next layer from the current one (the sky visible through the well's mouth) creates a standing goal (goal-gradient, Q1).
- Diegetic UI: door letters, floor numbers and clear conditions should live on the doors themselves (engraved plaques, glowing runes), and the "tutorial system" voice from the web novel can be a diegetic system window (blue translucent panels, the familiar web-novel "status window") that players already associate with the fantasy.
- Positional audio: Three.js PositionalAudio for monster cues, teammates' footsteps and proximity voice/text bubbles makes shared rooms feel inhabited and helps social deduction (hearing someone sneak away).

### Gaps
- The 2022 presence meta-analysis could not be read; I cannot report which specific design choices had the largest effect sizes.
- No study found that directly measures presence for stylized vs realistic art in party games.

## Q4. Which cosmetic categories sell best in party games, and what pricing and pass structures are considered fair in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Cosmetics that change how you are recognized (head/hair/hats, faces, full outfits) and how you express yourself to others (emotes and animations) dominate. The fairness bar in 2025-2026 is: cosmetic-only (no stat power), transparent real-money pricing, passes that do not expire (Helldivers 2 model), generous free earn rates, and no randomized paid rewards aimed at minors. Regulators (FTC vs HoYoverse, Jan 2025) now enforce odds disclosure and simpler currencies.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox marketplace (third-party trackers): hair is the largest category by transaction volume (about 28% of sales); faces have the highest per-item sales velocity (about 520 sales per item in 30 days); hats and headwear (crowns, beanies, horns, animal ears) are consistently popular; emotes and animation packs grew sharply after 2025, when creation access expanded to all UGC creators (about 10 of the top 30 trending items were emotes); the "Oldschool Animation Pack" is cited as the top seller with about 4.9M favorites. — [RoLearn: UGC marketplace trends 2026](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/roblox-ugc-marketplace-trends-2026/); [RoWatcher: Inside Roblox's UGC economy 2026](https://rowatcher.com/news/inside-roblox-s-ugc-economy-who-s-winning-in-2026); [GAMES.GG: Roblox UGC trends 2025](https://games.gg/news/roblox-top-ugc-trends-for-2025/) (third-party/aggregator figures; not verified against official Roblox data, and the exact page for each number was not confirmed)
- Fortnite cosmetic types sold in the Item Shop: outfits, gliders, emotes, back blings, pickaxes. — [Wikipedia: Fortnite Battle Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale); usage rankings of emotes exist at [Fortnite.GG most used emotes](https://fortnite.gg/most-used-emotes) (usage, not revenue)
- Fortnite 2025 economy change: V-Bucks became more expensive, and the Battle Pass price dropped from 1,000 to 800 V-Bucks while V-Bucks earned back from it fell (from 1,000 + 500 bonus to 800); players criticized this as paying the same money for less; Epic added 20% back in Epic Rewards for purchases via Epic's payment system in Fortnite, Fall Guys and Rocket League. — [Fortnite official news: V-Bucks price increase](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-v-bucks-price-increase?lang=en-US); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fortnite-v-bucks-are-getting-more-expensive/1100-6538682/); [ResetEra thread (player reaction)](https://www.resetera.com/threads/fortnite-v-buck-price-increase-effective-march-19th-battle-pass-down-to-800-v-bucks-previously-1000-bp-rewards-nerfed-to-800-from-1500.1458364/)
- Fall Guys F2P (2022): Show-Bucks premium currency, a Fame-based season pass, and Crowns moved to a separate cosmetic track; it reached over 20 million players within days of going free-to-play, but players criticized much stingier earn rates, with items once earnable by play now costing far more time or money. — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/news-fall-guys-goes-free-to-play-adds-show-bucks-season-pass); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fall-guys-season-pass-frustrations-fame-kudos-show-bucks/); [GameLuster opinion](https://gameluster.com/opinion-fall-guys-free-to-play-monetization-fall-from-grace/)
- Non-expiring battle passes (Halo Infinite first, then Helldivers 2 and Marvel Rivals) were widely praised as anti-FOMO; some players say it turned them into near-every-pass buyers. Caveat: Marvel Rivals passes persist only if bought during their season, while Helldivers 2 Warbonds remain purchasable permanently. — [PC Gamer: battle passes should never expire](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-and-marvel-rivals-convinced-me-battle-passes-should-never-expire/); [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/marvel-rivals-battle-pass-is-an-anti-fomo-win-that-doesnt-expire-at-the-end-of-seasons-theres-no-need-to-worry-about-not-having-enough-time/)
- NetEase on Marvel Rivals: "we will be charging for cosmetics that will provide only visual changes, with no stat bonuses or pay-to-win elements." — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/marvel-rivals-monetization-helldivers-2-battle-pass-permanent/); [ResetEra reaction](https://www.resetera.com/threads/marvel-rivals-battle-passes-do-not-expire-monetary-system-pricing-explained.1051950/)
- FTC vs HoYoverse (Genshin Impact), January 2025: $20 million settlement; alleged COPPA violations and deception about loot box odds (5-star items) and a confusing multi-tier virtual currency that obscured real costs. Required: block under-16s from buying loot boxes without parental consent, disclose odds and currency exchange rates, and offer direct real-money purchase of loot boxes instead of only via virtual currency. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/genshin-impact-game-developer-will-be-banned-selling-lootboxes-teens-under-16-without-parental); [FTC business guidance](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)
- Fall Guys shop rotation is tracked by fans daily, showing that rotating shops create a checking habit. — [fallguysstore.com](https://fallguysstore.com/) (fan tracker; evidence of behavior, not of revenue)

### Inferences (what this implies for our tower game)
- Shop category priority for a mascot party game: (1) headwear/hats (highest-visibility slot, cheap to make, stackable with any body); (2) full "mascot suit" outfits (Fall Guys-style themed costumes: monster costumes from bosses you have beaten, novel-inspired "Hell-difficulty" gear); (3) emotes and animation packs (victory dance at door clears, betrayal taunts, apology bows for after a betrayal); (4) faces/eye decals (the per-item velocity leader on Roblox); (5) party-specific signature items: door-opening effects, death/respawn effects, trails, name plates, "status window" themes. Death and door effects are high-visibility in a game where everyone watches each other fail.
- No power in the shop, ever: powers come only from play. This matches the NetEase statement and avoids the #1 player complaint (pay-to-win).
- Season pass: non-expiring and purchasable at any time (Helldivers 2 model); price near $5-10 equivalent; pays back enough premium currency to buy the next pass if completed (the pre-2025 Fortnite loop players valued); a substantial free track; progress from any mode, including hub minigames and failed runs.
- Currency: one premium currency with round, clearly shown real-money equivalents; always show the real price next to it. If any randomized reward exists (for example cosmetic "mystery doors"), make it earnable only (not paid), or disclose odds, add pity/duplicate protection, and gate purchase for under-16s, per the FTC settlement. Simplest ethical choice: no paid randomness.
- Rotation: a daily 4-6 item shop plus a weekly featured set creates a check-in habit without FOMO pressure if popular items return on a published cadence.
- Earn rates: be visibly generous at launch; the Fall Guys F2P backlash shows that reducing what play earns hurts sentiment even when player counts spike.
- Reward loops to use instead of loot boxes: deterministic collection (bestiary pages that unlock matching cosmetics), achievement cosmetics ("Cleared 3E without speaking") and co-op cosmetics unlocked only by teaming up (relatedness).

### Gaps
- No verified revenue share by cosmetic category for Fortnite, Fall Guys or Among Us (Epic and InnerSloth do not publish it); the Roblox numbers are from third-party trackers.
- Did not verify 2025-2026 loot box laws outside the US (for example Belgium, the Netherlands, the UK, Brazil); those should be checked before launch.
- No data on current Fall Guys pass pricing after the 2025 V-Bucks change.

## Q5. What are the red flags that make games feel unattractive or boring in the first session, and what do the first 10 minutes of hit games do?

### Takeaway
The biggest churn happens on day one: across popular mobile genres D1 retention is only about 30%. The usual causes are overloaded or forced tutorials, early failure the player does not understand, no visible reward or progress in session one, and store materials that do not show gameplay immediately. Hits put the player in control of the core fun within seconds and make failure social and funny.

### Cited Findings
- D1 retention across popular mobile genres is barely above 30%, so about 70% of users churn after one day; most churn happens on day one, and churn at the tutorial stage signals tutorial problems. — [devtodev: FTUE metrics](https://www.devtodev.com/resources/articles/main-metrics-ftue); a 2026 benchmark piece asks whether D1 is above 27% — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies)
- Cognitive overload (too many systems, info or decisions at once) is among the most common FTUE problems; force-teaching basics to players who already know the genre is itself a churn trigger. — [Playio blog: Onboarding decides your D1](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention) (practitioner blog)
- Players who fail in the first 60 seconds churn at much higher rates. — [Recognizing Patterns: FTUE, the 60 seconds](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/first-time-user-experience-your-player) (newsletter; no primary data shown)
- A meaningful reward plus visible progress inside the first session is claimed to add 5-10 percentage points of D1. — [Playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention) (claim without published dataset; treat as directional)
- High D1 with low D7 signals initial appeal but a lack of mid-game depth or meta-progression. — [Solsten: drivers of D1, D7, D30](https://solsten.io/blog/d1-d7-d30-retention-in-gaming); [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/how-to-increase-user-retention-and-increase-your-games-lifetime)
- Store-page red flags: trailers that open with CGI or cutscenes instead of in-game footage, too few distinct environments (reads as an asset flip), amateur capsule art. — [Game World Observer on Zukowski](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/03/11/steam-page-launch-guide-wishlists-zukowski)
- Juice experiments show that the same rules can feel dead or alive depending on feedback, so a flat, silent prototype reads as boring even with good mechanics. — [GDC Vault: Juice It or Lose It](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose)
- Monetization red flag at first contact: stingier earn rates and paywalled outfits generated immediate negative press for Fall Guys F2P. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fall-guys-season-pass-frustrations-fame-kudos-show-bucks/)

### Inferences (what this implies for our tower game)
- First-session red flags to avoid in our current design: (a) long text or system-window lore before control; (b) teaching powers, sabotage, betrayal, shop, hub minigames and layers all in session one; (c) a first room whose clear condition is too weird to guess (fail without understanding); (d) an empty or quiet room (no juice, no music, no other bodies); (e) lobbies waiting for humans (bots must fill instantly); (f) betrayal that deletes a new player's whole run (reads as griefing, not play); (g) no reward at the end of the first run.
- Proposed first 10 minutes:
  - 0:00-0:20: control a mascot on the well floor immediately; a diegetic system window says "Tutorial Floor 1. Choose a door." Three lettered doors glow.
  - 0:20-2:00: the first sub-level is a gimmick room with an obvious but funny clear condition (for example "Do not look at the statue": it moves only when watched), taught by a bot teammate demonstrating it. First codex stamp, first coin burst.
  - 2:00-4:00: the second door puts you in a room with 2-3 bots; a cooperative puzzle needs two players, so the first team-up comes early (relatedness).
  - 4:00-6:00: first power choice (pick 1 of 3; autonomy); the power immediately trivializes something satisfying.
  - 6:00-8:00: first social-deduction beat: a rival bot steals the clear credit or pulls a harmless prank (bounded betrayal that costs a little and is played for laughs); unlock a "revenge" emote.
  - 8:00-10:00: floor boss with a weird clear condition, then floor clear and results ceremony (4 meters fill; first cosmetic hat granted; pass tier 1 pre-stamped), then the camera pans up the well shaft to reveal the sky through the opening, with the text "Floor 2 doors: 0/10. Something is waiting in 2E." That leaves an interrupted task (Ovsiankina) and a visible bigger world (frog-in-a-well payoff).
- Delay the shop until after the first floor clear and open it with a free item; show the hub minigames as locked silhouettes to create future goals without overload.
- Track D1/D7 plus funnel events (first door, first clear, first team-up, first betrayal, first floor clear) to find where new players drop.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched that breaks down the first 10 minutes of Among Us, Fall Guys, PEAK or Lethal Company; the timeline above is a design inference built from the FTUE and SDT findings.
- Retention benchmarks cited are mobile-centric; browser/PC party-game benchmarks were not found.

### Design conclusions

1. **Every run fills 4+ meters on a results ceremony, with pre-stamped (endowed) bars, goal-gradient ordering and an interrupted task shown on the next launch (Q1).** SUPPORTED, with limits. The endowed-progress, goal-gradient and SDT evidence is solid. After the correction the Ovsiankina framing is actually the better-supported one. But all the progress studies are loyalty-card field studies, and the resumption data is from same-session lab tasks, so carrying them across days in a game is still an inference. Nunes & Drèze also tie the effect to the head start being seen as real progress toward the goal. A "2/10" Codex page stamped only by the tutorial should therefore be earned by a visible tutorial action, or it may read as a gift rather than progress.
2. **Toy-mascot avatar (rounded, stubby limbs, minimal face, big print surfaces) tested at real camera distance (Q2).** SUPPORTED. Developer sources back readability at small size, the "fail funny" goal, the customization canvas and simplicity for self-expression (PlayStation Blog by Kerins, Walsh in ESPN, Bromander). Two weak points: the "weeks of testing / humanoid too aggressive" story is unsourced (see claim 7), and "faceless = can't read liars" is fan opinion only.
3. **Responsiveness and juice before fidelity; third-person default with scripted first-person or close-camera "awe" shots (Q3).** Part SUPPORTED, part WEAK. Spending on responsive control and juice is well supported (SDT's link to intuitive controls, Juice It or Lose It, Screenshake), and the corrected presence study strengthens it, because agency affected presence more than perspective. The scripted first-person awe shots are WEAK. That same study suggests that taking control away for a cutscene-like camera lowers presence more than a 1PP view raises it. Keep the player in control during reveal shots, for example with player-steered camera pull-backs.
4. **Cosmetic-only shop, non-expiring pass purchasable any time, one transparent currency, no paid randomness (Q4).** SUPPORTED on ethics and sentiment (NetEase statement, PC Gamer/GamesRadar praise, Fall Guys backlash, FTC order). Two parts are WEAK. First, the takeaway that "regulators now enforce odds disclosure and simpler currencies" generalizes from one US consent order against one company; it is not a general US rule, and the non-US laws are unchecked (as the author notes). Second, a pass that fully pays for the next pass is a revenue choice with no evidence behind it. Fortnite itself cut that payback in March 2026, so it is not an industry-standard "fair" bar.
5. **Proposed first 10 minutes (door choice, gimmick room, forced team-up, power pick, bounded betrayal plus emote, boss, 4-meter ceremony, pass tier, layer reveal) plus a delayed shop (Q5).** WEAK. The timeline rests on mobile FTUE blogs whose key numbers are unverified (claim 23), and none of the cited hits' first 10 minutes were examined. It also conflicts with the section's own red flag (b), "teaching powers, sabotage, betrayal... all in session one". The plan brings in about 7 systems in 10 minutes: doors, gimmick rules, co-op puzzle, powers, betrayal, emotes and meta-meters. Suggest moving betrayal and the power pick to the second session, or making them skippable, and test with first-time players. The parts that are well supported are the delayed shop, bots filling lobbies instantly, and funnel tracking.
