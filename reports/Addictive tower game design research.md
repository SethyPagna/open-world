# Every Door Must Make You Stronger

Tower, system and death-game fiction and the games that borrow from it hook people with one engine: growth that is **visible, frequent and earned** and announced by a cold System voice, rules that tempt selfishness while hiding a clever way out, frustration paid off almost at once, failure that still pays, and a world that keeps revealing it is bigger than the arena. Our current design (the KNOTSPIRE GDD) kept the genre's surface (lettered doors, redacted clear conditions, Titles, a System that never lies) but **inverted its engine**: the hero's body shrinks all run, almost every verb costs health, powers are clamped so they never trivialise old rooms, a 36–45 minute run yields about **four picks** with the first arriving **4:40–7:10 in**, and a first-time player sees **0 of the 7 marked words** of the first room's objective while learning **22 bindings** and a betrayal layer of ten knife types, notes, bets and a tribunal. That is why it reads as clever and bureaucratic rather than fun, addictive or attractive. The highest-impact fixes, in order, are a visible power curve that pays out on every room clear and lets players steamroll old content, a plain and front-loaded first session, a results ceremony that makes every death pay, doors that advertise prize and danger instead of payout math, and one dramatic, contained betrayal verb in place of the social bureaucracy. A sponsor/audience layer is true to the source fiction but unproven in games, and it would add a system to a design already overloaded with them, so it belongs in a capped prototype rather than the core. None of the benchmark games publish retention data, so every timing target here is a hypothesis for a greybox test, and because the game is now a Unity build, the browser-era "one link, zero install" argument drops out while storefront, price and streamer tactics matter more.

## Status windows turn effort into numbers readers trust

Korean scholarship on game-fantasy web novels explains why the status window works. Kim Jun-hyun is a professor at Sungshin Women's University and a working web-novelist. He says the window does two jobs: it turns growth into numbers so readers see progress at once, and it gives the hero information nobody else has. The second job matters because, in his account, most web-novel readers cannot bear to watch the protagonist in real danger. He ties both to the pay-per-episode model, where each installment must pay off on its own ([SKKU News](http://www.skkuw.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=21866)). Moon Eun-hye's 2024 genre study finds that readers enjoy grinding second-hand when **goals are clear, procedures explicit and item and level rewards frequent**. The typical hero is a "sincere munchkin": overpowered, but diligent enough that the reward looks earned. The serial runs as a Crisis–Resolution–Crisis loop ([KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003134446)). English LitRPG authors describe the same pull. Travis Baldree talks about a steady drip of advancement moments. Will Wight says he has no patience for stories that waste his time. Pirateaba argues that progression without emotion goes flat, and emotion without progression stagnates ([Reactor](https://reactormag.com/the-rise-of-litrpg/)). The commercial ceiling is high. Solo Leveling's webtoon has **14.3 billion global views** ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/lifestyle/books/20240718/solo-leveling-franchise-expands-with-new-spin-off-webtoon)), and its anime won nine Crunchyroll Anime Awards in 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)). Dungeon Crawler Carl grew from a Royal Road serial to **over 14 million copies and 140 million Audible hours** by mid-2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Crawler_Carl)).

Korean readers use a pacing vocabulary for this: *goguma* (sweet potato, the choking frustration of a hero held back) and *saida* (cider, the fizzy release). In the National Institute of Korean Language's 2025 usage survey, **71.5% of respondents use "cider"** for a refreshing resolution and **56.8% use "goguma"** for a frustrating hold-up ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/culture/2026/02/06/goguma-means-frustration-cider-means-relief-korean-word)). Publishers advertise reading lists as having no goguma ([RIDI on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@ridi_storage/post/DKRFCSkR2ti/)). Craft guides treat every cliffhanger as a promissory note that must be cashed at the start of the next episode. They name four kinds of cut (crisis, twist, declaration, question) and advise varying them so readers don't go numb ([StoryBench](https://storybench.kr/blog/%EC%A0%88%EB%8B%A8%EC%8B%A0%EA%B3%B5-%ED%9A%8C%EC%B0%A8-%EC%97%94%EB%94%A9-%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0)). StoryBench is a practitioner blog, not research, but the rule matches how the industry markets itself. For a game, frustration works only as a short wind-up. A bad door, a trap or a betrayal needs a guaranteed chance to hit back within the next room. Every room should also end on a cut, such as the next door glowing or a hidden condition being detected, never on a menu.

Readers also separate earned growth from cheap growth. Reviewers say SSS-Class's copy-the-killer-and-rewind loop becomes predictable once the pattern is set ([ChapterBrief](https://chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-suicide-hunter-review)). The arc fans rank highest pits the same mechanic against a completely different power system ([ChapterBrief guide](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/sss-class-revival-hunter-reading-guide)). ORV's foreknowledge creates tension because it expires as reality drifts away from the novel the hero read ([ChapterBrief](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)). One LitRPG critic's essay (one blogger's view, not a survey) names heroes who beat their own stat sheets as the genre's worst habit, and recommends showing only stat changes rather than full sheets ([Dr. O](https://drosludochemistandnarratorium.wordpress.com/2022/02/04/the-problem-with-litrpgs-character-statistics/)). Two rules follow. Numbers must bind, so every upgrade changes what you can do in the next room. And new power should meet new kinds of problem, not just bigger versions of the old one.

Multiplayer breaks one assumption. The genre's pleasure is exclusive: Solo Leveling's hero is the weakest E-rank hunter, and the System lets only him level up ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)). With eight players, one player's cider is seven players' goguma. The multiplayer version therefore needs **per-player specialness** (each player holds a hidden class with its own odd unlock condition, so everyone is exceptional in a different way), **team-level cider** such as a whole-party rank-up, and **bots as the doubting onlookers** who sneer at the F-rank and gasp when it clears a Hell door. A tempting fusion, using the private status window as a social-deduction role card, needs playtesting rather than assumption: in Kim's account private information exists to relieve reader anxiety, whereas in social deduction it creates suspicion. Regression and "second life" premises are now called a classic rather than a trend, but Korean commentary reports reader fatigue, so novelty has to come from twists on the formula ([OhmyNews via Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20260415103745581)).

## Cold rules and watching gods manufacture cleverness and betrayal

The scenario template is simple. The System announces rules that make cruelty the easy route, and it hides a clever way out. ORV's first scenario, *Proof of Value*, is rated difficulty F. It orders subway passengers to kill at least one living creature within 30 minutes for 300 coins, on pain of death. The passengers turn on an old woman, until the hero crushes a grasshopper and shows that insects count ([ORV Wiki](https://omniscient-readers-viewpoint.fandom.com/wiki/Main_Scenario_1)). The Tutorial Is Too Hard (TTITH) supplies the same patterns in tower form. **Floor 3** hides an easy ground route in fog, and the floor manager tells the hero he cleared it in a weird way; **floor 16** traps climbers with five humans, one of them a doppelganger; **floor 61** makes the other boss weaker the more companions you sacrifice; and **floor 35** reverts you to your weakest body to re-walk the earlier gates ([TTITH Wiki: Floors](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/Floors)). Difficulty is chosen at the entrance (**Easy, Normal, Hard or Hell**), and harder tiers pay more ([TTITH Wiki: The tutorial](https://the-tutorial-is-too-hard.fandom.com/wiki/The_tutorial)). Each of these is already a playable room: an obvious clear, a hidden clear, and a selfish shortcut with a visible cost.

The audience turns style into reward. In ORV, constellations sponsor survivors who entertain them. A weaker but interesting survivor can therefore outgrow their base strength ([ChapterBrief](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/omniscient-readers-viewpoint-review)). ORV's English webtoon shows **596.5 million views and 4.5 million subscribers** ([WEBTOON](https://www.webtoons.com/en/action/omniscient-reader/list?title_no=2154)). TTITH's gods use the Tutorial to recruit apostles. Korean Wikipedia credits TTITH (2016) with starting the gods-as-observers trend, two years before ORV ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/튜토리얼이_너무_어렵다)). Dungeon Crawler Carl turns the audience into a staged reveal: nobody can watch floor 1 live, and viewer and follower counts switch on when floor 2 opens ([DCC Wiki: Ratings](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Ratings)). Its loot boxes can be opened only in safe rooms, all at once, lined up from Bronze to Celestial. The System AI may even skew loot for entertainment value ([DCC Database](https://www.dccdatabase.com/wiki/loot_boxes/)). DCC's comedy comes from the system itself: sarcastic achievements and absurd box names. The danger stays real. The risk for us is that if drama pays, griefing pays too. Payouts have to key on the room's own rules (hidden clears, clutch saves) and be capped, not count raw betrayals.

Death-game fiction builds its dilemmas into the rules rather than leaving them to spite. **Alice in Borderland** stamps each game with a suit that announces its type (Spades physical, Clubs teamwork, Diamonds logic, Hearts betrayal) and a number that sets difficulty and reward in visa days ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/alice-in-borderland-card-suits-represent/); [AiB Wiki](https://aliceinborderland.fandom.com/wiki/Visa)), which is a ready-made grammar for door plaques. **Liar Game's Minority Rule** eliminates the majority and breaks down once players announce their votes ([Cornell INFO 2040](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2022/11/03/liar-game-minority-rule-game-analyzed/)), so any vote room needs roughly five or more independent voters, with bots filling small lobbies. **Tomodachi Game's side-switch** lets a player defect to the winning team and pass their debt to the losers, but if the original team wins instead, the switcher swallows the losing team's average debt ([Tomodachi Game Wiki](https://tomodachi-game.fandom.com/wiki/Episode_7)); that penalty is what turns defection into a bet rather than a free option. **Tower of God's floor-2 Crown Game** runs five rounds in which the holder must stay on the throne, the winner passes and losers suffer nothing ([ToG Wiki](https://towerofgod.fandom.com/wiki/Crown_Game)), proof that competitive rooms can be low-stakes. And Tower of God's defining betrayal lands because trust came first. Rachel pushes Bam off during a test after faking that she couldn't walk to get his help ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/tower-of-god-unanswered-questions/)).

Scale reveals make the world feel vast. Sword Art Online's Aincrad has 100 floors. The producer of its 2026 game said depicting all of them would take about ten years, because each is roughly 10 km² ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/sword-art-online-echoes-of-aincrads-would-take-about-10-years-to-develop-if-the-devs-tried-depicting-all-100-floors-of-the-titular-world-according-to-series-producer/)). Cradle's hero fights his way out of a backwater valley into a far larger, more dangerous world ([T.L. Branson](https://www.tlbranson.com/cradle-books-in-order/)). Our Well → Sky → Space structure has exactly this shape. The source lesson is that each reveal should also open new options. SSS-Class's best arc worked because the setting change gave an old mechanic a new problem.

## Hades, DOORS and PEAK pay out every step and make failure funny

### Roguelites turn every step into a choice and every death into a deposit

Hades shows a reward icon over every exit, so choosing a door is choosing a build ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-symbol-meaning-guide-hera-dionysus-apollo-ares-aphrodite)). Each boon is a pick-1-of-3, and cross-god synergies keep runs fresh ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/hades-boon-replay-escape/)). Its runs last **20–45 minutes** ([KUTO](https://thelockoftime.world/blog/how-long-to-beat-hades)): long, but dense with decisions. Supergiant set out to take the sting out of dying, so that time spent never felt wasted, and tied story progress to each death ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). The team's mantra was that every run counts. Instead of a classic easy mode it built God Mode, which adds damage resistance with each death ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-hades-made-a-genre-known-for-being-impossibly-hard-accessible/)). Hades sold 1 million copies by September 2020, 700,000 of them in Early Access ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/hades-has-sold-1-million-copies-nearly-one-third-sold-in-last-few-days/1100-6482388/)).

Vampire Survivors turns in-run gold into permanent upgrades and keeps a long achievement list, so no run is wasted. Near-misses pull players back for one more try ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)). Its creator, formerly of the gambling industry, deliberately built an over-the-top chest animation with a catchy jingle ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/vampire-survivors-developer-interview/)). Balatro turns poker arithmetic into fireworks with layered animation, particles and audio ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro)).

"Broken builds" are not the lesson, though. Slay the Spire's GDC talk argues that every card should have its place ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025731/-Slay-the-Spire-Metrics)). In a 1–8 player betrayal game, one player's broken build can wreck everyone else's fun, so broken moments belong in co-op synergies and PvE numbers. Even hits decay: Slay the Spire 2 peaked at **574,638 concurrent players**, and its monthly peaks then fell to **105,197** by August 2026 ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/2868840)).

### DOORS proves the formula of one monster, one rule, one number

DOORS is the closest existing proof of our concept. It has 100 numbered hotel doors, and each rare entity has **one warning cue and one counter-rule**: flickering lights mean hide from Rush, never look at Eyes, turn around when Halt flashes TURN AROUND, move quietly past the blind Figure, and follow the Guiding Light during a Seek chase ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-doors-surviving-from-every-monster-guide/)). Deaths feel fair because the cue arrives before the danger and the rule can be learned. DOORS reached 1 billion visits in 94 days ([DOORS on X](https://x.com/DoorsRoblox/status/1591626621945094144)). It now has **7.79 billion visits** and a 93% rating, and its all-time peak of **381,720 concurrent players** came with the Hotel+ update on 29 January 2023 ([Rolimon's](https://www.rolimons.com/game/6516141723)). The August 2026 Archives update drew a spike of 226,937, about 59% of that record ([RoVitals](https://rovitals.com/game/6516141723)). Updates, in other words, are spikes on a declining baseline, not new records. Rolimon's lists DOORS' **average playtime at 11.56 minutes**, the only public session figure found for this genre ([Rolimon's](https://www.rolimons.com/game/6516141723)). The formula has spawned copies: Pressure peaked at 112,111 in March 2026 ([Rolimon's](https://www.rolimons.com/game/12411473842)). Neither game has tower growth, a System voice or betrayal. That gap is our differentiation.

### Co-op hits link players' fates and let friends supply the comedy

Caelan Pollock, who pitched PEAK, defines "friendslop" as games that assume friends will supply the comedy the game lacks ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-was-meant-to-be-friendslop-game-from-the-start)). The genre's shared traits are low barriers, team goals, proximity chat, physics and low prices, and these games are usually not much fun alone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop)). The comedy comes from three things together: **coupling** (my mistake hurts you), **visible blame** and **low failure cost**. Chained Together literally chains the team together ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_Together)) and peaked at 92,186 concurrent players ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/2567870)). PEAK's stamina bar shrinks with hunger, injury and carried weight, and its Scoutmaster punishes the climber who runs too far ahead ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_(video_game))). R.E.P.O.'s loot loses value when damaged, reviewers note that its slapstick reads even without audio ([CoopQueue](https://coopqueue.com/features/repo-vs-lethal-company/)), and it peaked at 271,571 players ([SteamDB](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/SteamDB/announcements/detail/539972907011409385)). Lethal Company became a viral hit largely through its players' intense shouting, per PC Gamer as cited on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company). This is a pattern read from successes only; no flops with similar coupling were checked.

Betrayal is fun when the rules announce it, rounds are short, a reveal lands and the victim keeps playing. Liar's Bar makes lying the rule, and a player caught lying plays Russian roulette ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar)). Among Us keeps dead crewmates doing tasks as ghosts, and any player can call a meeting and a vote ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)). A Schell Games designer's retrospective on Among Us 3D, anecdotal rather than a study, found betrayal funnier among friends, where breaking trust is expected and performed, and reports that naming a one-kill crewmate role "Vigilante" steered players toward talk of justice instead of griefing ([Digital Thriving Playbook](https://digitalthrivingplaybook.org/example/make-trust-break-trust-unlocking-prosocial-potential-in-among-us-3d/)). The honest counterpoint is that toxic is not the same as unpopular. Steal a Brainrot is built on stealing from strangers, and critics call it pay-to-win, yet it reached **25.4 million concurrent players** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). Our betrayal guardrails are therefore a wellbeing and brand choice, not an engagement maximiser. Dread Hunger and Deceit died of DDoS attacks, cheating and server costs, not of their betrayal design ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dread_Hunger); [Delisted Games](https://delistedgames.com/deceit-shutting-down-on-steam-on-november-6th/)).

The best-evidenced lever for a long tail is **new places plus timed events**. PEAK's all-time peak of about 170,000 came in the month of its Mesa update, not at launch ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/3527290)). Grow a Garden's concurrency records line up with named updates, peaking at 22.3 million on 23 August 2025, and the game ships weekly limited items ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)).

### Psychology explains why meters, juice and clear controls work

Self-determination research finds that autonomy, competence and relatedness each predict enjoyment and future play, and that the sense of presence tracks intuitive controls ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8)). Three field and lab effects explain why progress meters pull people forward. A head start helps: a car-wash card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-filled was completed by **34% of customers versus 19%** for an empty 8-stamp card with the same real requirement ([Nunes & Drèze](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23547282_The_Endowed_Progress_Effect_How_Artificial_Advancement_Increases_Effort)). People speed up near the goal: café customers bought faster as they approached a free coffee ([Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)). And people resume unfinished tasks: a 2025 meta-analysis found the famous memory advantage for interrupted tasks vanishes once the original 1927 data is excluded, yet people resume about **two-thirds of interrupted tasks** against a 50% baseline, though only in lab tasks within one session ([Ghibellini & Meier](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)). On immersion, a 2012 study found that **agency raised spatial presence more than camera perspective did** ([Behavioral and Brain Functions](https://behavioralandbrainfunctions.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1744-9081-8-34)). The GDC talk "Juice It or Lose It" made a Breakout clone compelling without changing a single rule ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose)). Roblox's own design docs warn that players who don't understand what to do in the first few minutes quit, and so do players bored by long prescriptive tutorials. The docs recommend teaching just in time ([Roblox creator docs](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques.md)). On character design, Fall Guys' designers gave the bean little legs so it trips and big arms so it is expressive when it falls ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/gaming/story/_/id/29734365/meet-minds-fall-guys-costumes)). They pushed its colours and tested costumes zoomed out at real on-screen size ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/05/25/creating-the-character-designs-of-fall-guys-out-on-ps4-this-summer/)).

## Our tower kept the genre's surface and inverted its engine

The table sets each part of the engine against what the current design specifies. The first-trait timing comes from the GDD's own timers: a 60 s warm-up, a 45 s Draft, a 6 s latch and a 2.2 s reveal. After that comes a room clear somewhere between the 50% hint and the 5:00 time-out, and then an Altar pick of up to 15 s.

| Engine part | What the hits and the fiction do | What the current GDD does |
|---|---|---|
| Growth rhythm | Hades offers a pick-1-of-3 boon and a choice of previewed exits; Vampire Survivors banks every run's gold as permanent upgrades | About **4 picks per 36–45 min run**; first trait at **~4:40–7:10**; filler cards such as "+20 wax" and "Blank"; many traits useless outside their home room ([GDD §9.2–9.3](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #25](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |
| Power fantasy | The underdog climbs past everyone; numbers bind | The body shrinks as wax burns; wax costs on almost every verb; "no permanent power"; "Levels never grant power"; old rooms clamp powers to L1 numbers while tiers add −24% timer and +32% monster speed ([GDD §5.1, §9.1, §9.9](../_design/GDD.md); [WORLD §3.1, §9.4](../_design/WORLD_STRUCTURE.md)) |
| First minutes | Roblox teaches just in time; DOORS gives one cue and one rule | **22 bindings** (25 keys); a 45 s Door Draft of 7-field plaques at the 60 s mark; a first-timer sees **0 of 7** marked Decree words ([GDD §4.1, §3.6, §6.0](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #34](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |
| Door choice | Hades shows the reward; Borderland's suit shows the type and the number shows difficulty | Plaques show capacity dots, a SPLIT/FINISH/SOLE icon, Omens and Notes; payout math makes a SOLE E door about 4.5× an A share, so optimal solo play sees **2 rooms per run** ([GDD §3.6](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #10](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |
| Betrayal | One betrayal announced by the rules, a reveal, and the victim stays in | 10 Knife types, Soot tiers, a Tribunal, truth-stamped Notes, bets and a Pact Pot; CRIT finds the betrayal math cannot produce the betrayals it promises ([GDD §7](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #14](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |
| Failure | A death pays out story, currency or codex progress | A Scorched room gives no reward and −25% max wax on the next floor ([GDD §5.3](../_design/GDD.md)) |
| Voice and audience | A sarcastic System, sponsors, proximity shouting | Sponsor layer: "There is none in the slice"; 24 fixed quick-chat phrases and no free text ([GDD §2.4, §7.9](../_design/GDD.md)) |
| Wow and world | DCC reveals the audience at floor 2; SSS's best arc changes the setting | The flagship reveal plays after a ~38 min run; layers are reskins by default ([WORLD §9.1, §10.2](../_design/WORLD_STRUCTURE.md)) |
| Meta | Hades' Mirror of Night, Vampire Survivors' gold, rank-ups | Cosmetics only (a Gilded skin takes about 29 runs); the Codex spoils rooms and is exhausted after about 2 runs ([GDD §11.5](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #11](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |
| Readability | Bright, pushed colours that read at small size | Dark rooms lit only by players; no monster tell is required to glow on its own ([GDD §2.2](../_design/GDD.md); [CRIT #28](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)) |

The pattern is consistent: **the design measures loss where the genre measures gain**. Wax is health and the body is the health bar. None of the six pillars mentions growth, reward or progress ([GDD §1.1](../_design/GDD.md)). The fiction calls the Tower a patient, bureaucratic examiner ([GDD §2.1](../_design/GDD.md)), and the systems follow suit. Non-room time (Landing, Intermission, Draft, Altar, waiting on the Tide) takes up 20–55% of each floor ([GDD §3.3, §5.4](../_design/GDD.md)), and the big payoff waits for the Crown. Run length alone is not the fault. Hades is a hit with runs of 20–45 minutes. The fault is how few meaningful gains fit into each minute.

The root cause is a habit of rigor. The team's previous project's README spends its space on SHA-256-verified asset packages, a 48-joint rig and 90 local checks, and never says what is fun ([TEAMBOUND_13 README](../TEAMBOUND_13/README.md)). KNOTSPIRE moved that rigor from physics into rules. The design's own critique puts scope at about twice the brief. Roughly **20–23 of its 34 findings** are contradictions, soft-locks or exploits between interlocking systems, and its fixes mostly add more rules ([CRIT](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)). The estimates of about 110 coined terms and about 45 systems were never counted, but the order of magnitude is right. Patching will make the design more correct without making it more fun. The GDD's rule is "Fun before physics" ([GDD §1.3](../_design/GDD.md)), and it needs a twin: fun before rules.

The raw material is good, though. The Wick is a bouncy candle with floating nub hands, a flame face and a belly-flop ([GDD §13.5](../_design/GDD.md)). It fits the toy-mascot findings, and a shrinking candle is funny and instantly readable. It only lacks a growing state. Gimmick monsters with non-combat clears are DOORS' winning formula. Regression Scars already make death teach. The Betrayal Cam (a 0.8 s slow-motion thread snap), the Trust Fall and the Golem shattering into bells are clip-grade reveals ([GDD §7.2, §6.1](../_design/GDD.md)). The concentric world has the same shape as DCC and Cradle. The pieces are right; they are pointed the wrong way.

## Eight ranked changes put power, clarity and payoff first

The ranking weighs impact on the brief's own words ("feel progress", "increase their powers", "addicting", "attractive") against the strength of the evidence and the cost.

| Rank | Change | Replaces in the GDD | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Visible, binding power curve.** A pick at every room clear, never filler; in-room pickups; a growing Flame level and corona beside fragile wax; System pop-ups that show each gain and its cause; drop the clamp so old rooms can be steamrolled | 4 picks per run, shrink-only body, clamped powers | Strong across the fiction and roguelites; the timing is untested |
| 2 | **Plain, front-loaded first session.** Play within 30 s; no Draft on floor 1; a plain first objective; move, jump and grab only; betrayal deferred to later floors | 60 s warm-up, 45 s Draft, redacted Decree, 22 bindings | Strong (Roblox docs, DOORS rule design); the mobile onboarding figures are unverified |
| 3 | **Every death pays, shown in a results ceremony.** Codex, bestiary and rank meters; Regression Scars as a warm death tip; the session ends on an unfinished task | No reward and −25% max wax on a Scorched room | Strongest (Hades, Vampire Survivors, the progress-meter studies) |
| 4 | **Doors show prize, type and danger.** A reward icon, a suit-like room type and a difficulty number; one run currency and one meta currency; formulas off screen | 7-field plaques, SPLIT/FINISH/SOLE math, 3 currencies | Supported (Hades, Borderland, CRIT #10 and #33) |
| 5 | **One betrayal verb, one reveal, contained cost, guaranteed revenge.** Knot and Cut plus the Betrayal Cam; one public vote; Knife types cut from 10 to 1–2 | Tribunal, Notes, bets, Pact Pot, Soot | CRIT itself supports the cut; the evidence that betrayal is funnier with friends is anecdotal |
| 6 | **Linked-body comedy and bright readability.** A shared-fate gimmick in every room, blame tags and replays, self-lit tells, opt-in proximity voice for friends only | Fixed quick-chat, dark by default | Supported by the pattern across hits, with survivorship bias |
| 7 | **Layers as level-ups and as live content.** Each layer adds a movement power, new room types and a new social rule; the next layer is teased at the end of floor 1; layers ship as named updates | Reskin-only layers; the reveal at minute 38 | Supported (PEAK, Grow a Garden, DOORS spikes) |
| 8 | **Tower host first, audience later, both capped.** Sarcastic System lines now; Watchers who tip for clever clears as a layer-2 prototype | "No sponsor layer" | True to the genre, unproven in games, and a griefing risk |

**The power curve comes first because it is the user's explicit ask and the design's deepest inversion.** Keep wax as the fragile health bar, but add a second axis that only grows: a Flame level with a visible corona, size and colour, so the silhouette shows gains and a trailer can cut from stub to inferno. Every room clear pays a pick-1-of-3, and none of the options is filler like "+20 wax" or "Blank". Traits need universal effects that stack, and cooperating players should earn Hades-style Duo combinations that make teaming up pay mechanically and betrayal cost something. Every gain appears as a small status-window message that names the change and its cause, never a full stat sheet. Old rooms should become satisfying to flatten, and difficulty should live in new rule types and higher floors. That is the payoff tower fiction promises and the clamp rule removes. Keep "no monster can be hit to death" as the identity of the clears, but add power displays: shatter small mobs, blast a slime into cold storage. A reasonable starting cadence is something visibly ticking every 30–90 seconds, a power choice at every room clear, and a rank or floor moment every 10–15 minutes. None of these intervals has a source. They are values for telemetry to settle, in the metrics-driven way Slay the Spire was tuned.

**The first session should teach doors, one-rule monsters and one power, and nothing else.** Room 1 should start within about 30 seconds of pressing Play. It should use a plain objective, a DOORS-style cue-then-rule monster and an automatic power pickup that needs no choice screen. It also needs a scripted slapstick beat. Floor 1 hides the Draft (auto-assigned, or two big picture doors). Redaction becomes an opt-in "decode it yourself for bonus loot" layer on repeat visits, not a wall on first contact. The bindings shrink from 22 to about 8 context-sensitive verbs, and player-facing proper nouns to about 25, with CRIT's naming collisions fixed. The fact-checked notes flag one trap. An earlier proposal to fit a door choice, a co-op puzzle, a power pick, a first betrayal, emotes and meta meters into ten minutes brings in about seven systems at once. The pick-1-of-3 choice screen and betrayal verbs belong after the first floor or in the second session. Keep a 30–40 minute full climb for committed groups. The default entry should be a short, dense climb whose first satisfying arc fits the roughly 10–15 minute sessions seen in DOORS-like games.

**Every death must deposit something, and the results screen must show it.** Borrow Hades' principle that every run counts and Vampire Survivors' permanent gold. A failed run fills several meters one after another: the door grid for the floor, bestiary pages for monsters whose rules you learned by dying, rank progress and a small currency. Order the meters so the one closest to full comes last. Start the first page with a head start the player has visibly earned in the tutorial, because the head-start effect depends on it reading as real progress. End each session on an unfinished task, such as a sealed door glimpsed or a rival bot that went ahead, and show it on the next launch screen. Regression Scars stay, delivered as warm death tips. Keep meta progression to two or three readable tracks: a Tower Rank from F to SSS shown in a ceremony, a small permanent talent tree, and per-player hidden classes with odd unlock conditions. Solo Leveling: ARISE shows why. Its no-gacha PC relaunch drew praise for its power-fantasy transformation, yet the reviewer still called the need to level up every single system unnecessary bloat ([Sypnotix](https://www.sypnotix.com/reviews/solo-leveling-arise-overdrive-review)). Tower of God: New World players complained about hard-to-get SSR teammates and growth resources until the developer eased acquisition ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/all-major-changes-coming-tower-god-new-world-reward-system-ssr-acquisition)). Meta unlocks should therefore be deterministic or pity-protected, and randomness should stay inside the run.

**Doors should answer two questions: what do I get, and how scary is it?** Put a Hades-style reward icon, a Borderland-style suit for room type (body, mind, together, heart) and a difficulty number on each plaque. Remove capacity dots, reward-rule icons and payout math from the plaque. Keep one run currency and one meta currency. Rare gold-rimmed doors and hidden bonus rooms add the variable reward that DOORS' random entities and DCC's box tiers provide. Keep them earned, never bought.

**Betrayal should be one legible verb with a contained cost and a guaranteed comeback.** Keep the Knot and the Cut, and make the Betrayal Cam the punchline at the room exit. Cut the Tribunal, Notes, Keyhole bets, the Pact Pot and Soot tiers, and reduce the ten Knife types to one or two. That matches what CRIT itself marks as beyond the brief. Losses stay inside the room or floor and never touch persistent power or skins. The victim stays in play as a ghost and gets a System-issued Vengeance quest in the next room: cider after goguma. Heart doors announce betrayal in their rules, as Liar's Bar does. Their templates come from the fiction: Tomodachi's side-switch with its failure penalty, TTITH floor 61's sacrifice-for-an-easier-clear, and ORV's cruel rule with a clever non-lethal answer. Vote-based rooms need five or more voters, with bots filling the gaps. Co-op stays the default and betrayal is the spice. Mark betrayers with an original infamy title, not a borrowed character name.

**Comedy and appeal come from linked bodies, visible blame and bright readability.** Every room needs one shared-fate gimmick: a tether, a shared oxygen pool, a fragile relic, or a curse where one player's jump moves another. Add a "who caused it" tag or short replay on every fall and every failed room. Monster tells and heroes should glow on their own inside dark rooms, with colours pushed and tested at real camera distance. Proximity voice belongs in friend lobbies only, as an opt-in. The GDD cut free text for safety, and voice raises the same risk further. Bots with named personalities are a reasonable bet for solo play, but no hit in the research gets its comedy from bots, so that bet needs testing.

**Layers should feel like leveling up and ship as events.** Each layer should open with a new ability that is on by default (glide in the Sky), new room types and a new social rule. The next layer should be visible from the end of floor 1, through a camera pull-back the player steers rather than a cutscene, because agency drives presence more than camera angle. Ship each layer as a named update with a new monster roster, and plan for spikes on a declining baseline rather than new records.

**The Tower's voice comes before its audience.** A sarcastic System that names achievements and roasts deaths costs little: the GDD already has System windows. It also adds the personality the design lacks, the way DCC's host does. Watchers and sponsors who tip for hidden clears and clutch saves are true to TTITH, ORV and DCC. They are still the largest system on this list and unproven in any game. They belong in a capped prototype that switches on at the second layer, as DCC's audience does, with payouts tied to room rules so griefing never pays.

**Cut the invisible engineering.** The Ghost Sim takeover, live Grate feeds, Keyhole spectating and the rising Draft maze consume budget the player never sees. CRIT calls the Ghost Sim state work the largest hidden cost ([CRIT #5, #20](../_design/GDD_CRITIQUE.md)).

**The move to Unity changes distribution, tooling and operations advice.** The notes assumed a Three.js browser game. Their "one link, zero install" advantage was already rated weak and no longer applies, and Three.js- and WebRTC-specific tips should be read as their Unity equivalents: engine physics for wobble, ragdolls and carrying or throwing friends, positional audio for monster cues, and a voice-chat middleware for proximity voice. If the build ships on Steam or a similar store, its store tools start to matter. Among 98 top sellers, 99% of capsules show prominent title text, 41% show a figure or creature, and about seven in ten read clearly at 120 pixels ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-capsule-trends-data)). Marketing guidance says trailers should open on in-game footage and show at least three distinct environments ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/03/11/steam-page-launch-guide-wishlists-zukowski)). Price for group purchase: PEAK's developer argued that $8 still reads as a $5 purchase ([Game File](https://www.gamefile.news/p/peak-interview)). Free access creates surges: Content Warning's free day on Steam drew **6.2 million claims in 24 hours** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/horror/april-fools-phenom-content-warning-draws-in-more-than-204000-concurrent-players/)). Seeding streamers works too: Fall Guys sent beta codes to streamers a day before its open beta ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2020/09/17/fall-guys)). Build server-authoritative betrayal actions and anti-cheat from day one, given how Dread Hunger and Deceit died. An auto-saved clip buffer and a "moment" button cost little in any engine.

**Aim for "addictive" without predatory hooks.** Timers do convert. Kakao modelled its wait-for-free timer on Anipang's energy hearts, and paid conversion rose from 3–4% to 25% ([Kakao](https://www.kakaocorp.com/page/detail/9349)). In a co-op game, though, per-player timers break a friend group's ability to play together, so keep daily quests and daily seeds and never gate play. Gambling aesthetics carry risk. Balatro was first rated PEGI 18 for gambling imagery before an appeal brought it down to PEGI 12 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro)). The US FTC's order against HoYoverse required odds disclosure and parental consent for under-16s buying loot boxes ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/genshin-impact-game-developer-will-be-banned-selling-lootboxes-teens-under-16-without-parental)). PCGamesN called Solo Leveling: ARISE one of 2024's worst gacha games after flash sales interrupted its story scenes ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/solo-leveling-arise/overdrive-relaunch)). Fall Guys' stingier earn rates after it went free-to-play drew immediate backlash ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fall-guys-season-pass-frustrations-fame-kudos-show-bucks/)). So: keep the jackpot feel of lights and jingles, sell only cosmetics, use no paid randomness, offer passes that don't expire, and keep earn rates generous. Payer rates will be low. WEBTOON's blended paying share is 4.6%, about 15% in Korea and about 1.5% in the rest of the world ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1997859/000199785926000015/a260303_2025q4xsharehold.htm)).

**Treat all of this as hypotheses until a greybox proves it.** None of the benchmark studios publish D1 or D7 retention. Every cadence number here is a judgment call. Several fiction details rest on fan wikis. The comedy pattern comes from successful games only. The cheapest test is a 10-minute greybox slice aimed at one feel target: every 90 seconds or so the player got stronger, laughed or saw something new. Run it as an A/B against a clamped version with no Flame level, and add funnel telemetry (first door, first clear, first power, first team-up, first betrayal, first floor clear) to see where new players drop.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. KNOTSPIRE does not lack features; it has too many, and its signs are reversed. The genre's pleasure is a number that rises for a reason the player can see, in a world that is bigger than it first looked. The current design shows a body that shrinks, a reward that waits for minute 38, and a world that grows while the hero's powers stay clamped. The first move is subtraction: cut rules, currencies, controls and nouns. The second is a change of direction: every door, death and layer should make the player stronger and show it. That order also protects the budget, because the cuts pay for the juice, rewards and rooms the player will actually notice.

Multiplayer is where this game can beat its sources. Novels and DOORS each have half of it. Tower fiction has growth, cruel rules and betrayal, but only one real protagonist. DOORS has fair one-rule monsters and a door counter, but no growth and no betrayal. A game where every player has a different secret specialness, the whole party can rank up together, and betrayal ends in a replay and a promised revenge turns fiction's single-hero pleasure into a shared one, and none of the games examined in this research combines those pieces. What stands in the way is not the concept but the current spec's instinct to explain every rule before letting players feel powerful.
